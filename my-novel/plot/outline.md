# Outline

*Updated through ch89. The story has grown far past the original Re:Zero-style small-arc plan into a sustained, escalating war narrative. This file now tracks what actually happened, plus where the author's original intentions may need re-checking against the current draft.*

## Premise (still holds)
A nuclear engineer, unwillingly isekai'd by a misfired Hero-summoning ritual, is not OP and starts weak — but he's a nerd who assesses situations clearly, and the shrine that fails to summon a real Hero treats him decently instead of dumping him. Tone still draws on Dr. Stone (constrained, materials-first engineering) and The Eminence in Shadow (quiet competence), but the story has darkened considerably past its original comedy/adventure pitch — see "Tone drift" below.

## What's actually happened, arc by arc

### Arc 1 — Selvane / The Shrine (ch01–05)
Complete, unchanged from earlier notes. Establishes personality, ethics, starting skillset, and the self-imposed rule against advanced weapons — **the rule this entire story is now about breaking.**

### Interlude — The Gathering Storm (ch06)
Complete. Demon Kin plan the Selvane strike.

### Arc 2 — Millbrook / The Ancient Cave (ch07–11)
Complete. Resolves the original "bigger problems" cutoff (a labor shortage, resolved through open engineering work) and delivers the planned Storage Box ability, reskinned as a soul-bound ring. Two named side characters (Fessin, Corwin) die here — the story's first character deaths, and the first time Leandro has to report an honest account of a mistake with real cost.

### Arc 3 — Ostwick (ch12–15)
Settling-in arc as originally planned — open engineering work, no secrecy needed yet. First mention of Seraphina by reputation.

### Turning point — The Fall of Selvane (ch16)
**This is the hinge of the entire story.** Matches the original plan (palace destroyed, learned as background news, guilt surfaces) almost exactly — except the emotional payoff is much bigger than originally scoped, because Voss (a named, personally-known mentor figure) dies heroically rather than the loss being abstract. This chapter is why the "shadow work" plan from the original outline evolved into something much more consequential: not quiet anonymous fixing, but eventually building a weapon.

### Arc 4 — Brindlemark dungeon (ch23–42)
A large addition not in the original plan. Functions as the "combat competence" arc — Leandro can't fight, so he goes and embeds with people who can, to understand what real capability actually costs and requires. Introduces Doran's party, who become central, beloved recurring characters (and later, the emotional fuse for the story's darkest turn).

### Arc 5 — Ravencourt / The Weapon Program (ch43–66)
**Not in any version of the original plan.** This is the story's biggest structural departure: Leandro, driven by Voss's death and unable to accept his own combat uselessness, designs and builds an explosive device explicitly analogous to a nuclear weapon (crystal "chain reaction," dragon stones as a stand-in for refined fissile material). Tests it once (erasing an island), then uses a field version to kill the Demon King in a solo infiltration raid (ch65). This directly and knowingly violates his own ch04 ethical line — the story does not flinch from this; it's treated as the central tragedy/tension, not a triumphant power-up.

### Arc 6 — Aftermath and Aldervine (ch67–86)
Political fallout from the Demon King's death (both sides), Bram's recovery, and a return to quieter reconstruction work in Aldervine — which doubles as the reveal (to the reader, not yet to Cassian) of Cassian's likely hidden noble past.

### Arc 7 — The Conspiracy (ch87–89, ongoing)
Doran's party is murdered by a war-profiteering noble network they'd stumbled onto. Leandro nearly commits to genocide-adjacent retaliation, is pulled back, and settles on a "more measured" targeted campaign — but is now lying to his own closest collaborators (Borgrim, Mirelle) about what he's actually building. **This is the live, unresolved thread the draft currently ends on.**

## Tone drift — flag for the author
The original brief was explicitly comedy/adventure, non-OP, "boring but revolutionary" engineering, with restraint and refusal-of-power as the character's core joke and virtue (the women's-bathroom gag, the "I won't build weapons" line). The story as drafted through ch89 has moved into much darker, war-tragedy territory: real named-character deaths (Voss, Fessin, Corwin, all of Doran's party), an actual weapon-of-mass-destruction arc, and a protagonist actively lying to people who trust him. This isn't necessarily wrong — it's a coherent, well-earned dramatic escalation, and the early comedy beats (Cassian's needling, the restraint gags) mostly stopped appearing organically once the war escalated. **Worth an explicit decision from the author:** is this intentional (the comedy was always meant to curdle into tragedy as the stakes rose), or has the story drifted further from the original comedy/adventure pitch than intended? Either is a valid call, but it's worth making consciously rather than by inertia, especially before continuing past ch89.
