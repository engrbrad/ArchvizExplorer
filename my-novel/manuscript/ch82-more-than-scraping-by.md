# Chapter 82 — More Than Scraping By

Leandro turned the number Cassian had quietly mentioned over in his head for the rest of that evening, and the longer he sat with it, the less it settled comfortably against everything he thought he understood about his closest friend.

"Cassian," he said, finding him alone later that night, cleaning his sword with the same unhurried care he always gave it. "How much, exactly, is 'more than I'd think'?"

Cassian glanced up, something carefully neutral crossing his expression before easy deflection took over. "Enough to help fund a town's reconstruction, like I said."

"That's not an answer. That's the same non-answer you gave earlier." Leandro sat down across from him, patient but unwilling to let it drop entirely. "I've traveled with you for over a year now. I've seen what you spend on gear, on drinks, on the ordinary costs of an adventurer's life. It never struck me as the spending of a man sitting on a fortune. You've always seemed—" he searched for the right word, "—comfortable, but not wealthy. Scraping by well, rather than sitting on reserves."

"Information brokering pays better than people assume," Cassian said, though something in his easy tone had gone slightly too practiced, too smooth.

"Enough to fund an entire town's reconstruction and still live comfortably for the rest of your life afterward?" Leandro pressed gently. "That's not information brokering money, Cassian. That's the kind of wealth merchant houses spend generations accumulating, or nobility inherits. I'm not accusing you of anything. I'm just—" he paused, choosing his words carefully, "—surprised. And a little concerned that there's something about your own history I've never properly understood, despite everything we've survived together."

Cassian was quiet for a long moment, setting his sword aside with deliberate care, something unreadable working behind his usually open, easy expression. "There's things about where I come from I haven't talked about," he admitted finally, voice quieter than his usual confident register. "Not because I don't trust you. Just because it's old, and complicated, and I made my peace with leaving that part of my life behind a long time before I ever met you."

"You don't owe me an explanation," Leandro said carefully. "I'm not asking to pry into something you've deliberately left behind. I just want you to know I noticed, and that whatever it is, it doesn't change anything about how much I trust you."

Cassian studied him for a long moment, something complicated and grateful passing behind his eyes. "I appreciate that," he said quietly. "More than I can easily say." He picked his sword back up, resuming his careful maintenance with hands that weren't quite as steady as before. "Someday, maybe, I'll tell you the whole of it properly. Not tonight. Not while we're all still recovering from everything the last six weeks put us through. But someday."

"Whenever you're ready," Leandro said simply. "I'm not going anywhere."

"No," Cassian said, something almost fond beneath the lingering weight of whatever he wasn't yet ready to share. "I don't suppose you are, at this point."

They let the conversation rest there, the mystery of Cassian's true origin settling, unresolved, into the same quiet category as the dragon stones and the invasion's true orchestration — threads Leandro filed away with patient, careful attention, trusting that time, and Cassian's own readiness, would eventually bring the full truth to light when it mattered most.
