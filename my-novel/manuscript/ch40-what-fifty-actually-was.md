# Chapter 40 — What Fifty Actually Was

It was Ilyana who found the anomaly, exploring the golem chamber's far end while the rest of the party finished distributing their haul — a passage that, according to every guild record and prior rumor about this depth, shouldn't have existed at all.

"This isn't in any of the reports," she called back, staring into a wide, unnaturally bright opening that led not into another stone chamber, but into something that looked, disorientingly, like open sky.

The party gathered at the threshold, staring out at a landscape that had no business existing fifty floors beneath solid earth — rolling terrain stretching toward a horizon lit by something that mimicked genuine sunlight, forests and hills and what looked, distantly, like the ruins of some ancient structure half-swallowed by wild growth.

"Floor fifty was never the end," Doran said slowly, processing the sight with the same disbelief written across every face in the party. "It was the last dungeon floor. This is something else entirely."

They advanced cautiously, and the difference in scale announced itself almost immediately — a beast crossing the distant treeline that dwarfed even the giants they'd fought two floors prior, moving with an unhurried, apex confidence that suggested it feared nothing currently within its territory. Something shrieked overhead, wheeling shapes far larger and more numerous than the harpy flock they'd barely survived. The very air carried a different weight, a pressure Leandro's instincts, honed now across months of careful danger assessment, immediately flagged as considerably beyond anything the previous fifty floors had demanded.

"This isn't built for six people," Percival said quietly, watching a second massive shape emerge from the far treeline, larger still than the first.

"It's not built for sixty," Bram said, uncharacteristically grim. "Look at the scale of this place. Whatever's out there isn't guarding a chamber. It's populating an entire territory."

Doran studied the open terrain for a long, careful moment, the same measured calculation he brought to every decision weighing visibly behind his eyes. "We're not equipped for this," he said finally. "Not today. Possibly not for a long while yet, however much we've grown getting here."

"This needs an army, not a party," Ilyana agreed. "Multiple full teams, proper coordination, maybe guild-level backing given the scale of what we're looking at."

Leandro stared out at the impossible landscape, feeling the same restless, analytical hunger he'd felt standing at the edge of the original cave entrance months ago — not reckless eagerness, but the genuine, careful curiosity of a man confronting a system whose true scope he'd only just begun to glimpse.

"Fifty floors," he said quietly, "and it turns out that was just the entrance exam."

"Poetic way to put it," Cassian said, equally subdued, staring out at a horizon that promised danger on a scale none of them, even now, fully understood.

Doran turned back from the threshold, expression settling into quiet, resolved decision. "We document what we've found. We report it to the guild, properly, with everything we've learned. And we come back—" he glanced around at his exhausted, battered, but very much alive team, "—when we're ready, and not a moment before. This isn't a floor to rush."

Nobody argued the wisdom of that. They retreated from the threshold, the strange, sunlit expanse sealing itself from view as they withdrew, leaving behind a discovery that would, Leandro suspected, reshape Brindlemark's entire understanding of its own dungeon the moment word reached the guild hall.
