# Chapter 65 — Launch

He made his final approach in the deep, quiet hours before dawn, when even a heavily garrisoned fortress's vigilance ran thinnest, guiding the glider with careful, focused precision toward the central keep — the same obsidian chamber, he suspected, where Vharek Dromm had once calmly planned Selvane's fall.

What he hadn't expected, drifting silent and unseen above the fortress's dark rooftops, was how quiet the whole place felt. Seraphina's intelligence had warned him the Court's seated generals moved constantly between fronts, campaigns, and scattered operations — but seeing the evidence of it firsthand, watching only a modest garrison patrol the walls below rather than the concentrated might he'd half-expected, settled a strange, uneasy clarity into his plan. Whatever waited inside that black stone hall tonight, it likely wasn't the full Court.

He set the glider down on the fortress's highest rooftop with careful, silent precision, unclipping his harness the moment his boots found solid stone, moving fast now, guided by nothing but Seraphina's description of the keep's layout and his own instinct for where such a structure's most significant chamber would sit.

He found the rooftop access point above what he judged to be the central hall — thick, ancient stone, but the geometry matched everything he'd studied of the obsidian chamber's likely position beneath. He knelt, working quickly, setting the device against the stone with the same careful, deliberate hands he'd used in every prior test, checking the timer twice before finally, irrevocably, activating it.

He didn't linger to confirm what waited below. He returned to the glider, relaunching from the rooftop's edge with a running leap that carried more desperation than grace, wind magic straining every reserve he had left as he fought for altitude and distance in the same motion.

He made it perhaps half a mile, glider catching a favorable downdraft that carried him out over the fortress's outer defenses, when the sky behind him turned violently, impossibly bright.

The shockwave reached him before the sound did, a wall of displaced air that caught the glider broadside and sent it tumbling out of any controlled flight entirely. Leandro felt the frame shudder and crack around him, silk tearing loose from its rigging, and understood, with the same clean, clinical detachment he applied to any failing system even now, that he was no longer flying so much as falling.

He hit the mountainside hard, the glider's wreckage absorbing some of the impact before disintegrating entirely around him, and rolled, weightless and disoriented, down a steep slope of loose scree that scraped and battered him the entire way before finally, mercifully, depositing him in a shallow gully at the base of the ridge.

He lay there for a long moment, ears ringing, vision swimming, cataloguing his own condition with the same instinctive triage he'd applied to every injury since arriving in this world. Alive. Ribs — bruised, possibly cracked, breathing painful but functional. Leg — sprained, not broken, weight-bearing with effort. Head — no loss of consciousness, minor concussion likely.

He was, he realized slowly, sitting up with considerable effort, right at the edge of the blast radius — close enough that the shockwave had nearly killed him outright, far enough that the fortress's own defensive walls had apparently absorbed and redirected the worst of the actual detonation's force before it reached his position.

He turned, wincing against the pain in his ribs, and looked back toward the fortress.

Where the central keep had stood, there was now a smoking, scorched crater, the surrounding walls collapsed inward in a wide radius of devastation, fires already catching in the wreckage despite the pre-dawn darkness. It was, even from this distance, clearly and unmistakably the death of whatever had occupied that obsidian hall.

He didn't know, in that moment, whether he'd killed a general, a garrison commander, or something else entirely. He wouldn't learn the truth — that the Demon King himself had been holding a rare, quiet audience in that very chamber, the seated Court's generals scattered across a dozen fronts and campaigns far from their sovereign's side — until word filtered back through Seraphina's contacts weeks later, delivered with the particular disbelieving shock of a continent processing news too enormous to fully comprehend all at once.

For now, he simply sat in the gully, bruised and bleeding and profoundly alive, watching the fortress burn from a safe, accidental distance, and allowed himself, finally, a single, exhausted breath of something close to relief.

I'm alive, he thought. And whatever that was, it's not getting back up.
