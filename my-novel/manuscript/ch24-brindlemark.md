# Chapter 24 — Brindlemark

The guild hall, when they finally found it past two wrong turns and one very insistent street vendor trying to sell Leandro a "genuine artifact" that was, on closer inspection, a bent spoon, turned out to be nearly three times the size of Selvane's — a sprawling, multi-level building clearly expanded piecemeal over years to accommodate a population of adventurers that had long since outgrown its original footprint.

The registration clerk, a harried young man juggling what looked like a full week's backlog of paperwork, barely glanced up as he processed their transfer. "Names, current rank, home guild."

"Leandro Valdez, D-rank, Selvane," Leandro said, and watched the clerk's pen pause, just slightly, at the name of the fallen capital.

"Selvane," the clerk repeated, something more attentive entering his tone now. "You'll want to speak with Guildmaster Orrin before you take any contracts. He'll want it noted personally, given—" he didn't finish the sentence, but didn't need to.

They were shown to a modest office on the hall's upper floor, considerably less grand than Vharek's obsidian war table but no less purposeful, papers stacked in careful, functional order across a desk behind which sat a broad-shouldered man well into his fifties, gray threading through a beard that had clearly seen better-kept days during less chaotic weeks.

"Selvane transfers," Orrin said, not quite a question, studying them both with the particular assessing look Leandro was beginning to recognize as universal among competent guild leadership. "You knew Voss."

"We did," Leandro said. "He promoted me himself, a few months before the fall."

Something shifted in Orrin's weathered face — grief, carefully controlled, and beneath it, something that looked almost like pride. "I trained under him, thirty-some years back, before I ever ran a guild hall of my own. Wasn't flashy about anything he did, not once, in all the years I knew him. Just relentlessly, unglamorously thorough, in a way most young adventurers mistake for dullness until it saves their lives one day and they finally understand what they were actually looking at."

"That's a fair description," Cassian said quietly.

Orrin nodded slowly, something private and heavy passing behind his eyes before he visibly set it aside, the way a man does when grief needs to wait for a more private hour. "I'm not half the swordsman he was, and I've never pretended otherwise — my own rank's respectable enough to run this hall credibly, nothing more. But I'll say this plainly, for both of you, since you clearly understood the man better than most passing through here will ever get the chance to: what he did at that gate wasn't an accident of circumstance. That was thirty years of a man refusing to let his own competence go soft just because nobody was watching closely enough to demand it of him. I'm proud to have learned anything from him at all." He cleared his throat, businesslike again, though the warmth didn't fully leave his voice. "Your ranks transfer as recorded. Welcome to Brindlemark. It's louder than Selvane, considerably less organized, and I'd wager you'll both find it either delightful or maddening within the week. Possibly both."

Cassian hadn't undersold the town's chaos, but what he'd left out, Leandro discovered over the following days, was the sheer density of genuine capability hiding beneath it. Brindlemark drew adventurers precisely because its dungeon was real, active, and dangerous enough to matter — which meant that beneath the daily theater of fires, arguments, and suspiciously cheap "artifacts," the town also hosted a concentration of serious talent unlike anywhere Leandro had yet traveled.

"That's a B-rank party," Cassian said one afternoon, nodding toward a group moving through the market with the unhurried confidence of people who'd stopped being impressed by their own reputation years ago. "And that—" he tilted his head toward a quieter table near the guild hall's entrance, four figures who somehow made the surrounding noise feel like it was politely keeping its distance, "—is Brindlemark's local pride. Full A-rank party. Cleared deeper into that dungeon than anyone else currently registered here, last I heard."

Leandro studied them with open curiosity — a swordswoman with the kind of stillness that suggested she rarely needed to move fast because she rarely needed a second attempt, a mage whose aura alone made the surrounding air feel faintly charged, a heavily armored figure built like a walking fortification, and someone at the table's edge who seemed unremarkable until Leandro noticed how carefully the other three deferred to whatever he said.

"They're strong," Leandro said, more observation than question.

"Strongest independent party this town's got, unless someone's passing through," Cassian confirmed. "Guild practically builds its dungeon safety protocols around whatever floor they've most recently confirmed clear." He nodded toward Leandro's own registration card, tucked away now in his pack. "For reference — since I don't think I've ever actually told you outright — I sit at B-rank myself. Have for a few years now."

Leandro blinked, genuinely caught off guard. "You've never mentioned that."

"Didn't come up," Cassian said, with the particular ease of a man who'd never felt the need to lead with his own credentials. "Wasn't relevant to fixing wells or hauling refugees out of a burning capital. But it's relevant here, a little — this town runs on rank the way Selvane ran on reputation. Figured you should know before someone else told you first and made it sound more impressive than it needed to be."

"It sounds plenty impressive as it is," Leandro said honestly, recalibrating his understanding of his friend with the same careful attention he gave any new data. B-rank, he was coming to understand, wasn't nothing — it placed Cassian meaningfully above the loud, chaotic majority crowding Brindlemark's streets, closer to the town's genuine elite than Leandro had ever quite appreciated.

"I've had a while to get there," Cassian said, with a shrug that undersold it further. "Point is — between the sword you helped me find and whatever rank I've already got, I'm not exactly dead weight if we end up somewhere dangerous. Wanted you to actually know that, rather than keep assuming I'm just good company and a decent bluff."

"Noted," Leandro said, filing it away with the quiet, growing appreciation of a man slowly realizing his closest companion in this world was considerably more capable than his easy manner ever advertised.

It was nearly two weeks into their stay, after a modest string of low-risk contracts meant purely to get a feel for the town's particular rhythms, that the coincidence occurred.

Leandro and Cassian had made their way to the dungeon's outer approach — more scouting than serious attempt, Leandro wanting to see the entrance properly before committing to anything — when the same A-rank party from the guild hall arrived at the same entrance, mid-conversation about a floor they intended to push past that day.

"Twice in two weeks," the swordswoman said, noticing them with open, easy curiosity rather than the wary distance Leandro half-expected from adventurers so far above his own rank. "You two thinking of trying your luck down there?"

"Scouting, mostly," Leandro admitted. "Wanted to see what we're actually considering before we commit to anything foolish."

"Sensible," the unremarkable-looking leader said, studying them both with more attention than his quiet demeanor suggested. "Rare quality around here, if I'm honest."

Cassian, never one to let an opportunity pass quietly, brightened considerably at the exchange. "If you're headed in anyway — mind if we tag along the first stretch? Nothing ambitious. Just want my friend here to see the real thing before he goes drawing up floor plans from secondhand descriptions alone."

The swordswoman exchanged a glance with her party, some silent, practiced communication passing between them, before she shrugged, easy and unbothered. "Early floors are simple enough for us at this point. Wouldn't be much trouble having you along, so long as you keep pace and don't wander off chasing something you can't handle."

"We can manage that," Cassian said, grinning, already falling into step beside them with far more enthusiasm than Leandro's own more measured curiosity, though he found himself, despite his caution, genuinely eager to finally see the thing they'd traveled all this way to study up close.
