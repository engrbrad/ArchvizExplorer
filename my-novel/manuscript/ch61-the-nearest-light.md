# Chapter 61 — The Nearest Light

They moved fast, faster than exhaustion or wounds should have reasonably allowed, carrying Bram between them in a desperate rotation while Leandro maintained what emergency treatment he could manage on the move — the bleeding controlled, but Bram's color draining steadily paler with every mile, his consciousness slipping in and out despite his own grim, silent effort to stay present.

"There's a garrison town two hours out," Seraphina said, checking her own memory of the region against the fading daylight. "Small, but it should have at least a field healer stationed there given the front's proximity."

"Two hours might be too long," Leandro said, voice tight, watching Bram's breathing grow shallower with every passing mile.

"It's the nearest option we have," Doran said grimly. "We make it work."

They pushed the pace beyond safe or sustainable, Cassian and Doran trading off carrying Bram's weight when his own legs finally gave out entirely, Ilyana running ahead to warn the garrison of their approach, Percival maintaining a thin, exhausting stream of minor healing magic that did little beyond keeping Bram's blood loss from accelerating further.

They reached the garrison's outer gate with the light nearly gone, Bram barely conscious, his breathing dangerously shallow, and found Ilyana already waiting with a priest pulled hastily from evening duties, expression tight with professional urgency the moment she laid eyes on the wound.

"How long since the injury," the priest demanded, already moving, hands glowing with the particular focused light of emergency triage magic.

"Nearly three hours," Leandro said.

"That's close. Very close." The priest worked fast, magic flooding into the ruined stump with visible, immediate effect, Bram's labored breathing finally, gradually steadying beneath her focused effort. "Blood loss this severe, this long without proper treatment — another half hour, maybe less, and I wouldn't have had enough to work with."

The tension that had carried them across two brutal hours of forced march finally broke all at once, Ilyana sinking to her knees beside Bram's stabilizing form, Doran's shoulders sagging with released relief he'd clearly been holding back through sheer force of will.

"He'll live," the priest confirmed, finishing the emergency stabilization with careful, practiced hands. "The limb itself — I can't regrow that here. This is field triage, not proper regenerative magic. He'll need a specialist, someone trained specifically in restoration work, and that's going to cost considerably more than anything this garrison can offer."

"We'll find that specialist," Leandro said immediately. "Wherever they are. Whatever it costs."

"I know a few names," Seraphina said quietly, still catching her own breath. "Academy-affiliated healers who specialize in exactly this kind of work. It won't be quick, and it won't be cheap. But it's possible."

Bram, conscious now if barely, managed a faint, exhausted smile despite everything. "Wasn't going to let that thing take Percival's head off," he said, voice weak but steady. "Arm seems a fair trade, all things considered."

"We're getting it back," Ilyana said fiercely, gripping his remaining hand. "Whatever it takes."

They rested at the garrison for two full days before continuing on, Bram stable enough to travel but still requiring careful, patient care, the dragon stones sitting quiet and secure in Leandro's storage ring throughout — a victory paid for at a cost that settled heavy over the entire party, tempering whatever triumph they might otherwise have felt at both the general's fall and the vault's contents finally, successfully secured.

"Three dragon stones," Cassian said quietly, sitting beside Leandro during a rare, quiet moment of the garrison's recovery period. "A dead Court general. One friend who's going to need months of proper healing before he's whole again."

"That's the actual cost of what we're building," Leandro said, watching Bram sleep fitfully nearby. "I don't think I fully understood that, not properly, until today."

"You knew the risk going in."

"Knowing and feeling aren't the same thing," Leandro said quietly, echoing words he'd once offered Cassian himself, a lifetime ago, after Voss's death. "I understand that a great deal better now than I did this morning."

They set out for Ravencourt's territory three days later, the dragon stones secured, Bram healing steadily under Percival's continued care, and Leandro carrying the weight of everything the search had cost alongside everything it had finally, successfully achieved.
