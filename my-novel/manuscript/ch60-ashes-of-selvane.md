# Chapter 60 — Ashes of Selvane

The ruins announced themselves long before the party crossed into what had once been the capital's outer walls — a haze of ash still hanging faint in the air months after the fire, the particular, heavy silence of a place that had once held tens of thousands of lives and now held only wreckage and scavenging crows.

Seraphina had insisted on coming this far after all, unwilling to let them walk into contested, unstable ruins on nothing but secondhand information she herself had gathered. "I can requisition a few days' leave from the pass," she'd said, before they departed. "My replacement can hold the line that long. I'm not sending you into Selvane's wreckage on my research alone and staying safely behind while you pay for any gaps in it."

Nobody had argued the offer away. Her presence, more than anyone's, changed the calculus of what the party could survive.

They found the palace district by mid-afternoon, the once-grand structure reduced to collapsed stonework and blackened support beams, the vault buried somewhere beneath rubble that had clearly resisted whatever scavengers had already picked through the more accessible ruins.

It was Ilyana who spotted the first sign of trouble — fresh tracks in the ash, too numerous and organized to belong to ordinary scavengers. Moments later, the ruins confirmed it, soldiers closing from three directions in a coordinated ambush that made immediately clear this wasn't opportunistic raiding.

"Formation!" Doran shouted, and the party collapsed into their practiced shape as the first wave closed distance.

He appeared from the collapsed remains of the palace's grand entrance — tall, armored in something that turned aside Percival's wind-enhanced strikes without visible effect, moving with terrible, patient confidence.

"Survivors, wandering this deep into contested ground," he said, voice carrying an unhurried cruelty. "I am Threxis, Eighth Seat of the Court of Twelve. A poor place to test your luck, little humans."

The fight that followed was unlike anything the party had faced, even with Seraphina's fire joining the engagement from the opening exchange — Threxis moved with speed and power that made every previous threat feel gentle by comparison, weathering Bram's shield-guard with contemptuous ease, shrugging off Percival's amplified magic with only a shallow, closing wound to show for it.

Seraphina's flame, still carrying the enhanced output of Leandro's bracelet, forced the general to actually respect the engagement for the first time — a wall of concentrated fire driving him back several steps, buying Doran and Ilyana the opening to press from both flanks.

"He's fast," Seraphina called, already circling for a better angle, sweat beading despite the heat radiating from her own spellwork. "Faster than anything I've fought since the pass. Don't get isolated!"

It was, despite her warning, Bram who found himself isolated moments later — Threxis reading the shield-bearer's defensive positioning and turning, with sudden, focused fury, directly toward him rather than the more immediately threatening casters.

"Bram, move!" Doran's warning came a half-second too late.

Bram threw himself into the strike's path anyway, shield raised in a desperate block meant to buy Percival, caught mid-cast just behind him, the seconds he needed to finish a spell. The claw tore through shield and armor both, and his scream — the first sound of real pain any of them had heard from the quiet, steady giant — tore through the chaos with horrifying clarity.

"BRAM!" Ilyana's voice cracked entirely, watching him stagger back, his shield arm gone below the elbow, blood pouring freely.

"Get him back!" Leandro was already moving, water drawn desperately from the scorched ground, forming a crude pressure bandage even as his hands shook. "Seraphina, cover the gap!"

She didn't hesitate, flame surging to fill the space Bram's fall had opened, buying Cassian and Doran the seconds needed to drag him fully clear.

"He needs a proper healer, now," Leandro said, voice tight, applying every scrap of emergency knowledge he possessed to a wound beyond anything his training had prepared him for. "I can stop the bleeding. I can't regrow a limb."

"Then we finish this first," Doran said grimly, turning back toward the fight with blood streaming from a dozen shallow wounds of his own. "Fast."

With Seraphina's fire anchoring the engagement properly for the first time, the fight's shape finally began to shift. Her flame, relentless and amplified beyond anything Threxis had clearly anticipated from a human caster, forced him to divide his attention in a way Bram's shield and Percival's wind alone never had — and it was that division, that fraction of a second where his focus split between the mage scorching his flank and the swordsman closing from his blind side, that finally opened the wound the party needed.

Doran took it, light magic flaring at its absolute limit, a precise strike driving into the general's exposed side with everything he had left. Threxis roared, staggering, dark blood — unmistakably not human — flowing freely for the first time in the engagement.

"Now!" Seraphina's voice cut through the chaos, fire building to something considerably beyond her earlier output, the bracelet's amplification finally pushed to its genuine limit. "Everyone, together—"

Cassian's blade found the same wound next, shifted to its longest reach, driving deep with a strike carrying every ounce of growth he'd earned since a desperate bear encounter months ago. Ilyana followed a heartbeat later, precise and merciless. And Seraphina's final, concentrated blast of amplified fire tore through the same critical point with enough force to finally, decisively end it.

Threxis fell, the Eighth Seat of the Court of Twelve collapsing into the ash and rubble of the capital his own forces had helped destroy — and the ruins fell silent, broken only by the ragged, exhausted breathing of six people and one very tired mage who had, together, barely survived.

"That," Percival said, breathing hard, staring at the fallen general, "was not a fight we win without you here."

"None of us win it alone," Seraphina said, equally spent, already turning toward Bram's still form with urgent concern overriding her own exhaustion. "We don't have time to appreciate that properly right now. He needs help."
