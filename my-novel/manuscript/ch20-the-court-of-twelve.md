# Chapter 20 — The Court of Twelve

Far north, beyond the Fangs, in the black-stone hall where the assault on Selvane had first been planned, General Vharek Dromm stood once more before the war table — except now, for the first time in longer than most present could remember, an empty seat sat among the twelve carved thrones ringing the chamber's upper dais.

The Ninth Seat's absence hung over the gathering like a held breath nobody wanted to acknowledge directly.

"An unfortunate loss," said the general who occupied the Twelfth Seat — youngest of the Court, arrogant in the specific way of someone who had never once, in his considerable lifespan, faced a fight he didn't win comfortably. He lounged rather than sat, one clawed hand idly tracing the rim of a goblet he hadn't touched. "Though I confess I struggle to summon much grief for it. The Ninth was capable, but predictable. Whatever human found a way to kill him likely did so through some fortunate accident rather than genuine strength."

"You weren't there, Kaelthorn," Vharek said, flat and unamused.

"No," the Twelfth Seat — Kaelthorn — agreed easily, entirely unbothered by the correction. "But I've read the reports. A guild functionary. Some minor human official who'd spent thirty years pushing papers and occasionally swinging a sword at bandits. Hardly the stuff of legend, however the humans wish to dress up his corpse in their little broadsheets."

Serai, seated closer to Vharek's position, spoke up before the general could respond himself. "The report also states you were wounded in that same engagement, my lord Twelfth. Badly enough to withdraw before finishing him."

Something flickered behind Kaelthorn's easy composure — brief, and gone quickly, replaced by a dismissive wave of his hand. "An inconvenience. The human was already dying on his feet by the time I arrived — the Ninth had worn him down considerably before falling. I merely finished delivering a mercy he'd already earned through his own persistence. Nothing more."

Vharek studied the younger general for a long moment, saying nothing, filing the deflection away with the same patient calculation he applied to every piece of information that crossed this table. He had not read the report the way Kaelthorn clearly hoped he had — not as a footnote confirming an easy kill, but as a warning half-buried in careful, self-serving language. A guildmaster, already exhausted from felling one of the Twelve outright, had still managed to wound a second badly enough to force a withdrawal. That was not the shape of an "inconvenience." That was the shape of a species Vharek had, perhaps, underestimated in ways his own Court seemed determined not to examine too closely.

"We will discuss the Ninth Seat's replacement in time," Vharek said instead, choosing not to press the point in front of the full Court. "For now, the campaign proceeds as planned. Selvane has fallen. The humans are scattered, reorganizing, weaker than they have been in a generation. We continue."

The meeting moved on to logistics, to the next targets along Althea's fractured border, to the slow, patient grinding-down of a kingdom that had already lost its capital and didn't yet know how much further the pressure intended to reach. Kaelthorn said little more, though Vharek noted, with the same careful attention he gave everything, the faint stiffness in the young general's posture — a wound, perhaps, healing slower and less cleanly than his dismissive words suggested.

Interesting, Vharek thought, watching him. Whatever actually happened in that street, you understand it was closer than you want anyone here to know.

He said nothing about it aloud. Some information was worth more, held quietly, than spent proving a point to a room full of proud, ambitious rivals who would use any admitted weakness as leverage against him rather than as a warning worth heeding.

The Court adjourned without further incident, eleven generals now instead of twelve, one seat sitting empty at a table that had not, in the arrogance of its remaining members, yet learned to properly fear what it was fighting.
