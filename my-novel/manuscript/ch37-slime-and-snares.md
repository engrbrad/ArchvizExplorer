# Chapter 37 — Slime and Snares

Floor forty-nine's danger revealed itself more gradually than any previous floor — a wide, unassuming chamber whose floor was littered with dozens of gelatinous, faintly translucent slimes, individually unthreatening but concealing, Leandro noticed almost immediately, an unsettling number of subtle irregularities in the surrounding stonework.

"Traps," he said, crouching at the chamber's edge before anyone advanced further. "This floor's not dangerous for the slimes. It's dangerous for what they're sitting on top of."

His caution proved well-founded. The slimes themselves fell easily to almost any sustained attack, splitting and dissolving with minimal resistance — but their sheer numbers, combined with a floor riddled with pressure plates and concealed pit traps clearly designed to punish careless footing amid the creatures' distracting presence, made simple, direct engagement a genuine liability.

"They want us focused on the slimes," Leandro said, mapping the visible trap pattern with the same patient attention he'd once given the ancient cave's sealed chamber. "While the actual floor does the real work underneath us."

It took considerably longer than the chamber's modest threat level alone would have suggested — careful, methodical clearing, Leandro identifying safe paths while the rest of the party dispatched slimes in controlled, limited bursts rather than wading in recklessly. Cassian nearly triggered a collapsing floor trap mid-fight, saved only by Leandro's sharp warning and his own quick, practiced reflexes redirecting his weight at the last possible moment.

"That would have been an ugly way to go," Cassian said, breathing hard, staring down at the now-visible pit his misstep had nearly opened beneath him. "Death by slime chamber. Wouldn't have made much of a story."

"Which is exactly why we're being careful," Leandro said, already tracing the next section of safe path forward.

By the time they cleared the chamber fully, the fight itself had cost them little in the way of direct injury, but the sustained mental strain of constant vigilance left everyone nearly as exhausted as the giants' brute physical demands had.

"Different kind of tired," Doran observed, rubbing at his temples. "I'd almost rather something try to kill me outright than spend three hours worried the floor itself might do it instead."
