# Chapter 84 — The Court, Waiting

Beyond the Fangs, in the fortress that now served as the Court's primary seat since the old stronghold's destruction, Vharek Dromm continued the delicate, exhausting work of holding a fracturing command structure together through sheer patient will.

The consolidation had held for months now — no major offensive, no significant loss of ground on either side, an uneasy stalemate that suited Vharek's cautious instincts considerably better than it suited generals like Rethgar, whose barely restrained frustration simmered louder with each passing Court session.

"This paralysis serves no one," Rethgar said, again, at yet another gathering that had begun to feel, to Vharek, less like strategic deliberation and more like a recurring test of his authority's remaining strength. "The humans rebuild while we sit idle, chasing ghosts and mysteries that may never resolve."

"The humans have made no significant advance either," Vharek said evenly. "Whatever caution costs us, it costs them equally. Patience is not weakness, Rethgar. It is the same discipline that has guided this Court's strategy since long before either of us held our current seats."

"Discipline that got our King killed," Rethgar snapped, and this time, several other generals shifted uncomfortably rather than immediately dismissing the accusation, the months of unresolved mystery clearly wearing on more patience than Vharek's alone.

It was Threll, careful and precise as always, who redirected the mounting tension before it could fully erupt. "I have new intelligence," she said, drawing the table's attention. "Fragmentary, but worth the Court's consideration. My informants report unusual activity among certain human noble houses — financial movements, correspondence patterns suggesting coordinated interest across multiple kingdoms, all converging on a single, consistent theme."

"Which is?" Vharek asked.

"Ensuring this war continues," Threll said. "Actively working against any faction, human or otherwise, that might seek its resolution. I don't yet understand their full motive, or how deep this coordination runs. But it exists, and it predates even our King's death by some considerable margin."

The chamber fell into a different kind of silence than Rethgar's earlier challenge had produced — not tension, but genuine, unsettled surprise.

"You're suggesting," Vharek said slowly, "that some faction among the humans themselves has actively worked to prevent this war's end, regardless of the cost to their own people."

"I'm suggesting the evidence points that direction," Threll said. "I would not stake the Court's strategy on it without further confirmation. But it would explain a great deal that has puzzled me for some time — why certain diplomatic overtures floated quietly through minor channels years ago never reached any serious consideration, why human resource-sharing proposals that might have eased tension before open war ever began were dismissed so uniformly across kingdoms that otherwise share little coordination."

Malgorath, silent through most of the exchange as always, finally spoke, something sharper than his usual indifference coloring his low, unhurried voice. "That," he said, "would explain considerably more than any theory this Court has produced about our own King's death."

Vharek looked to the Second Seat with fresh attention. "You believe the two are connected?"

"I believe," Malgorath said slowly, "that I have spent months troubled by an absence of any strength I should have sensed in whatever destroyed our stronghold. Perhaps I was searching for the wrong kind of answer entirely. Perhaps this was never about strength at all — theirs or ours. Perhaps it was about someone, human or otherwise, with reason enough to want this war's current shape preserved, regardless of who currently commands either side of it."

The Court sat with that unsettling possibility for a long moment, the mystery that had haunted them for months shifting, suddenly, into a shape none of them had previously considered — not an unknown, terrifying power to fear, but a hidden, patient hand manipulating the war's continuation for reasons that had nothing to do with conquest at all.

"Find out more," Vharek said finally, to Threll. "Carefully. If there is truth to this, I want to understand it fully before this Court commits to any further action based on assumptions we can no longer trust."

The meeting adjourned with more genuine unease than any prior gathering since the King's death — a Court built on the certainty of raw strength and patient strategy, suddenly confronting the possibility that the true architect of their current suffering might never register on any sense they'd ever learned to rely upon.
