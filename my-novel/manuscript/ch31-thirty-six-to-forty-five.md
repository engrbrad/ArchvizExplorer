# Chapter 31 — Thirty-Six to Forty-Five

The dungeon's character shifted again past floor thirty-five, swarm density giving way to fewer, considerably larger threats — creatures the size of the three-horn bear that had once nearly killed Leandro outright, though these moved with a coordinated intelligence their wild cousins back in Althea had never displayed.

It was, unexpectedly, exactly the kind of fight Cassian had spent years training for.

Floor thirty-six's guardian — a massive, horned beast plated in chitinous armor along its flanks — charged the party with the same brute, singular force Leandro remembered from his own near-death encounter months earlier. But where that fight had ended in Leandro's unconscious body and a bear finally brought down by Cassian's desperate, exhausted effort, this one unfolded like something closer to a dance.

Cassian moved first, closing distance rather than retreating, sword shifting mid-stride into its longer, spear-like form to meet the charge head-on rather than dodge it — a decision that would have been suicidal months ago, and now, watching him execute it with total confidence, looked instead like exactly the right call from a man who'd finally grown into the strength his rank had always promised.

"He's gotten faster," Ilyana observed, genuine surprise coloring her voice as she held back rather than immediately engaging, watching Cassian redirect the beast's charge with a precise, angled block that sent it stumbling past rather than through him.

"He's gotten better," Doran corrected, equally impressed. "Speed's part of it. That's technique."

Cassian pressed the opening without hesitation, blade shifting again mid-combo into its shorter, faster dueling form, a rapid series of strikes finding gaps in the creature's armor that Leandro suspected, watching closely, came from months of quiet, private practice Cassian had never made a show of. The beast staggered, favoring one side, and Bram finished what Cassian's opening had started with a single, decisive blow that ended the fight in barely two minutes total.

"That," Percival said, "was A-rank work. I don't care what your guild card says, Cassian. That's not B-rank technique."

Cassian, breathing hard but grinning wide, sheathed his sword with visible satisfaction. "Might be time to ask the guild for a reassessment, then."

"Might be," Doran agreed, studying him with new, genuine respect. "Been a while since I've seen someone grow that much between one expedition and the next."

Leandro watched the exchange with quiet, proud satisfaction of his own — not surprise, exactly, since he'd noticed the change building gradually over the preceding floors, Cassian's movements growing sharper, his reads on enemy patterns faster, the sword responding to him with less hesitation each time he called on a new form. But seeing it confirmed so plainly by people whose own combat judgment carried real weight settled something warm in his chest that he didn't examine too closely.

"You've been training," Leandro said quietly, once they'd made camp that evening.

"Every night I could manage it, since the cave," Cassian admitted, turning the sword over in the firelight. "Wasn't going to let you be the only one getting stronger through sheer stubborn effort. Figured if you could learn earth magic from nothing but frustration and a muddy canal wall, I could manage a few extra hours with a blade that already wanted to work with me."

"It shows," Leandro said simply.

Floors thirty-seven through forty continued the same pattern — larger, more dangerous individual threats rather than overwhelming swarms, and Cassian meeting each one with steadily growing confidence, the sword's shifting forms becoming an extension of instinct rather than a tool he had to consciously call upon. By floor forty, Doran had stopped hesitating before deferring the front-line engagement to him on anything short of the party's strongest members, a shift in trust that spoke louder than any guild rank reassessment could.

"We should be more careful from here," Doran said, studying the passage ahead as floor forty gave way to forty-one. "We're close enough to forty-six now that I want everyone sharp. Whatever's nesting down there hasn't let anyone past it yet for a reason."

"We've got the antidotes," Leandro said. "Ferrin's formula, tested and reliable. I stocked enough for the whole party, several doses over."

"Good," Doran said. "Because floor forty-six isn't going to care how far we've already come, if we walk in unprepared."

The party pressed on, floors forty-one through forty-five passing in a tense, careful rhythm of increasingly dangerous but manageable fights, every one of them acutely aware of the growing proximity to the wall that had stopped every expedition before them.
