# Chapter 3 — The Offer

A month passed in a rhythm Leandro found almost comforting, in a strange way — mornings with Yolanda in the courtyard, afternoons with Rasmin going through Althea's history, evenings spent alone doing what he'd always done best: writing things down, organizing what he'd learned into something resembling order.

His water magic came along slowly, the way anything worth learning did. He couldn't do much — a controlled ripple, a small suspended sphere the size of a fist that took real concentration to hold, occasionally directing a thin stream from one container to another instead of just pushing it around blindly. Yolanda measured his progress not in flashy displays but in a rank system she explained plainly the second week, once she'd decided he was serious enough to bother.

"Everyone with usable magic is classified," she'd said, sketching a rough scale in the dirt with a stick. "Novice. Apprentice. Adept. Expert. Master. It is not about raw power alone — control matters, efficiency matters, how well you can apply what little or much you have. A Novice with excellent control can outperform an Apprentice who wastes half their strength fumbling."

"So it's not a hard ceiling," Leandro said. "It's a floor you can build up from properly."

"Precisely." She'd looked almost pleased he'd framed it that way. "You, Leandro Valdez, are a low Novice. Possibly the lowest I've measured in years, in raw affinity." She'd said it without malice, the same clinical tone he appreciated in himself. "But your control, for someone one month into practice, is unusually clean. You do not waste effort. That will matter more than affinity, in time."

He'd taken that the way he took any honest performance review — noted, filed, used as calibration for what to work on next.

By the end of the month, he could reliably draw water from a nearby source, hold it, and move it with deliberate, if unspectacular, precision. Not impressive. Not useless, either. A tool with a narrow but real range of function, same as the man wielding it.

The summons came on a quiet afternoon — Rasmin and Yolanda both waiting for him in the shrine's modest study, along with a third figure Leandro hadn't met: a well-dressed man introduced simply as a representative of the crown, here on behalf of interests Leandro suspected went well above a village shrine.

"You've made remarkable progress," Rasmin opened, "for someone with your... starting conditions."

"I had good teachers," Leandro said, glancing at Yolanda, who allowed herself a small, satisfied nod.

The crown's representative got to the point faster than the other two had over the past month. "The kingdom would like to offer you a position, Master Valdez. A modest one, agreed — advisor to the royal engineers, perhaps, or a research post within the mage academy given your... methodical mind. Room, board, a stipend. Protection, should anyone take undue interest in an outsider with unusual origins."

It was, Leandro understood immediately, a genuinely good offer. Safety. Stability. A place in a system that already, mostly, made sense to him. A month ago, disoriented and stripped of every certainty he'd built his life around, he might have taken it without hesitation.

He didn't take it now.

"I appreciate it," Leandro said, and meant it. "Genuinely. You didn't have to treat me the way you have. You could have thrown me out, or worse, and you didn't. I won't forget that."

"But," Rasmin said, reading the shape of the sentence before Leandro finished it.

"But no." Leandro folded his hands, the same posture he used for delivering an unpopular but necessary conclusion in a meeting. "I've spent a month learning about a world I still barely understand secondhand. Kingdoms I haven't seen. Monsters I've only heard described. A Demon Kin threat I know only as a paragraph of history, not a lived reality. If I take a desk position now, I'll spend the rest of my life being an outsider's theory of usefulness — advising on things I've never actually seen, based on secondhand accounts filtered through people who have their own interests in how they're described to me."

"You want to see it yourself," Yolanda said. Not a question.

"I want data I collected myself," Leandro said. "Not data I was handed. That's not distrust of either of you specifically. It's just how I work. I don't sign off on a system I haven't personally inspected."

The crown's representative looked faintly scandalized, the way career bureaucrats tended to look when someone declined stability in favor of an unscheduled variable. Rasmin, to his credit, looked almost like he'd expected it.

"You intend to travel," Rasmin said.

"I intend to travel," Leandro confirmed. "Modestly. Carefully. I'm not interested in glory, and I have no illusions about how far low-Novice water magic gets me against something dangerous. But I'd like to see villages, cities, the parts of this world that ledgers and history lessons can't fully capture. If I'm going to be useful to anyone — including myself — I want to understand what I'm actually working with first."

There was a pause. Then Rasmin, unexpectedly, smiled.

"That," he said, "is the most sensible thing anyone summoned into this shrine has said in fifty years."

The crown's representative did not look nearly as pleased, but didn't argue further — perhaps sensing, correctly, that arguing with Leandro's logic was a losing proposition. Before the meeting closed, Rasmin offered one more thing: supplies, a modest coin purse, a letter of introduction bearing the shrine's seal that would, at minimum, keep him from being treated as a vagrant or a suspicious drifter in Althea's cities.

And, almost as an afterthought, a name.

"There is someone I'd suggest you meet, before you go," Rasmin said. "A man who knows the roads better than any of us confined to this shrine. Untrustworthy in the ways that don't matter, and reliable in the ways that do. He's been known to... take an interest in outsiders."

Leandro had a feeling, even before the name was said, that this was going to be the buddy he'd been half-expecting to show up eventually.

"His name," Rasmin said, "is Cassian."
