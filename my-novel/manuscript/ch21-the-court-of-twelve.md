# Chapter 21 — The Court of Twelve

The war table's map, updated now with fresh markers since the Court's last full gathering, showed activity along three fronts — Selvane's ruins in the east, quiet border pressure along the northern passes, and, in the west, a cluster of disturbances that had, until this meeting, drawn considerably less attention than the capital's fall.

"The Fourth Seat," Vharek said, turning to a broad-shouldered general seated near the dais's western curve, his presence marked by an unsettling stillness that made even the more boisterous members of the Court instinctively lower their voices when addressing him. "Your western operations. Report."

The Fourth Seat — Morvain, called the Wolf of the Western Reach by those who survived encountering him and lived long enough to name him — inclined his head slightly, voice a low, measured rumble. "Proceeding as intended. I've spent the better part of two months seeding disruption through the western territories — corrupted beasts, twisted migrations, engineered incidents designed to look like natural monster activity rather than deliberate campaign. Ravencourt's borders, and the wilder territories beyond them, have been kept occupied chasing symptoms while remaining blind to the cause."

"To what end?" Kaelthorn asked, some of his earlier bravado returning now that the conversation had moved away from his own uncomfortable withdrawal. "Selvane was the target. Why waste effort scattering distractions elsewhere?"

"Because Selvane was never meant to stand alone as a message," Vharek said, before Morvain needed to answer for himself. "A single capital's fall can be mourned, reorganized around, absorbed by a kingdom determined to recover. But a continent kept constantly occupied — resources spread thin chasing manufactured crises in a dozen directions at once — cannot properly regroup around any single loss. That is the purpose of the Fourth Seat's work. Not conquest. Distraction. Ensuring that whatever strength the humans might otherwise mass in response to Selvane remains scattered, exhausted, and looking the wrong direction."

Morvain's stillness shifted almost imperceptibly — something that might, on another face, have been the ghost of satisfaction. "The humans have already taken the bait, as it happens. Ravencourt's academy dispatched one of their strongest independent assets to deal with what they believe is an unusually aggressive migration pattern along the western wilds. A fire-affinity mage, quite formidable by human standards, entirely occupied chasing symptoms I've been careful to keep just severe enough to justify her continued attention."

Vharek's attention sharpened slightly at that, though he kept the reaction carefully contained. "The same mage the humans speak of in their taverns? The one they've taken to calling by some dramatic epithet?"

"The same," Morvain confirmed. "Currently three weeks' hard travel from Selvane's ruins, and likely to remain occupied for some time yet, provided I continue managing the disruptions at the pace I've established. She is skilled — more skilled than the beasts I've thrown at her thus far would suggest, and I confess a portion of my effort now goes toward ensuring she doesn't grow suspicious of the pattern before I judge the distraction to have served its purpose."

"You sound almost respectful," Kaelthorn said, a faint sneer creeping into his tone. "Of a human."

"I respect competence wherever I find it," Morvain said, entirely unbothered by the implied insult. "It is why I remain occupying her attention with carefully calibrated threats rather than simply sending something strong enough to end her outright. A dead obstacle stops mattering. A distracted one continues serving my purpose indefinitely, so long as I manage the leash correctly." He paused, something almost thoughtful entering his measured tone. "Though I will say — she is beginning to notice patterns in the migrations that shouldn't exist naturally. I estimate two months, perhaps three, before she either concludes someone is engineering the disruptions deliberately, or grows frustrated enough to abandon the chase and return east regardless. I will adjust as needed."

Vharek nodded slowly, filing the timeline away with the same careful precision he applied to every piece of the larger campaign. "See that you do. The last thing this Court needs, with Selvane already drawing more organized human attention than any incursion in a generation, is your western distraction resolving itself before it's served its full purpose."

"Understood," Morvain said, and the matter closed with the same quiet efficiency that had apparently characterized his entire operation thus far — invisible, deliberate, and, unlike Kaelthorn's earlier bravado, entirely unconcerned with being noticed or praised for it.

Vharek studied the map a moment longer after the discussion moved on, noting, with private unease he kept carefully from his expression, the growing list of variables no longer fully under the Court's control — an empty Ninth Seat, a Twelfth Seat nursing a wound he refused to properly account for, and now a human mage growing steadily more capable of recognizing manufactured chaos for what it actually was, however successfully Morvain currently managed to keep her leashed to the wrong conclusion.

Comfort makes people slow, he had told this very Court, months ago, confident in the humans' complacency.

He found himself, quietly, wondering how much longer that confidence would hold — for the humans, or for his own increasingly overconfident Court.

The meeting continued, moving down the ring of thrones as Vharek called on each seated general in turn — a formality he maintained less out of respect for protocol and more because it forced every ambitious mind in that chamber to account for their contributions in front of eleven rivals, a habit that had, over years, done more to keep the Court's internal politics honest than any punishment he could have devised.

"The Sixth Seat," Vharek said next.

The general who answered — a slight, unassuming figure named Threll, easy to overlook among the Court's more physically imposing members, which Vharek suspected was rather the point — inclined her head with a thin, knowing smile. "My work does not lend itself to dramatic reports, General. I have spent the better part of three years placing assets within human territory — servants, merchants, minor court officials, the occasional guild clerk. Nothing that draws attention. Nothing that could be traced back to any single source if discovered."

"To what purpose, presently?" Kaelthorn asked, some of his earlier arrogance returning now that the conversation had safely moved past his own uncomfortable withdrawal.

"Presently, listening," Threll said. "Selvane's fall has thrown every neighboring kingdom into a state of reactive panic — which court factions are consolidating power, which nobles see opportunity in a weakened Althea, which merchant houses are quietly repositioning their loyalties. All of it useful, eventually. None of it urgent enough yet to act upon directly." She paused, something colder entering her thin smile. "Though I will note, for the Court's benefit, that Ravencourt's academy has begun making inquiries about the pattern of monster activity in their western territories that goes beyond simple concern for their dispatched mage. Someone there is asking the right kind of questions. I don't yet know who, or how far those questions have traveled."

Morvain's stillness sharpened slightly at that, though he said nothing directly, and Vharek noted the small, silent exchange between the two generals with interest — Threll's information a quiet warning shot across her fellow general's carefully managed distraction, delivered with just enough professional courtesy to avoid open accusation.

"Keep listening," Vharek said simply. "Report anything that moves from inquiry to action."

"Of course, General."

He moved down the ring further, calling names, receiving answers that ranged from the concretely useful to the deliberately, carefully vague — the Third Seat reporting slow, patient work corrupting a supply route feeding Althea's remaining eastern garrisons, details withheld "until the method proves itself, rather than risk premature countermeasures"; the Seventh Seat offering little beyond a confirmation that his own forces remained in reserve, position and purpose unstated even to his fellow generals, which several present clearly found more unsettling than reassuring; the Eleventh Seat speaking only briefly of "personal research" that he declined to elaborate on further, waving away Kaelthorn's needling questions with the bored indifference of someone who considered the Twelfth Seat's curiosity beneath his attention.

Vharek allowed the vagueness where it served the Court's larger interests — a general who revealed nothing risked nothing being intercepted or countered before it bore fruit, and he had learned, across a long and careful career, that patience with his own subordinates' secrecy often paid dividends he wouldn't have predicted at the time. But he filed each evasion away nonetheless, the same careful cataloguing he applied to every variable on the board, human or otherwise.

By the meeting's end, the map bore fresh markers across half the continent — confirmed operations, half-revealed schemes, and outright unknowns that even Vharek, presiding over the whole grinding machine of the campaign, couldn't yet fully account for. Eleven generals remained where twelve once sat, each pursuing the slow unmaking of human civilization through methods as varied as the personalities behind them — brute conquest, patient distraction, quiet infiltration, and secrets kept close enough that even allies within the same Court didn't fully trust each other with the shape of what came next.

Vharek found something quietly troubling in that fact, turning it over long after the Court had dispersed and the black stone hall sat empty and silent once more.

A weapon this varied is difficult for any enemy to predict, he thought, studying the map's scattered markers alone. But it is equally difficult for its own wielder to fully control.

He said nothing about the thought to anyone. Some truths, he had long since learned, were safer held alone than spoken aloud, even — perhaps especially — among one's own allies.
