# Chapter 68 — Closed Borders

The retreat, when it came, didn't look like victory up close.

Reports from the front described it plainly enough — Demon Kin forces pulling back from contested territory, consolidating into denser, more heavily defended positions rather than the sprawling, patient advance that had characterized the campaign since Selvane's fall. But consolidation, Seraphina's contacts made clear, didn't mean weakness. If anything, the concentrated troop density along the Fangs and the newly reinforced border stretches had grown considerably heavier than before, checkpoints tightened, patrols doubled, entire stretches of contested ground now bristling with a defensive density nobody had seen since the war began.

"They're spooked," Seraphina said, laying out the latest intelligence across the workshop table. "Nine generals left, no king, and something erased their capital stronghold without warning. Of course they've pulled in tight. A frightened enemy consolidating isn't a beaten enemy. It's a more dangerous one, in some ways — cornered, uncertain, watching for the next attack from every direction at once."

"Which means the border's effectively sealed," Cassian said grimly, studying the map's newly marked checkpoints. "Nobody's getting through that without serious credentials, or serious force behind them."

"Which means Leandro—" Mirelle started, then stopped herself, unable to finish the thought.

"Which means if he's out there, he's on his own for longer than any of us want to admit," Borgrim said, voice rough. "No way for us to mount a search through that kind of lockdown without drawing exactly the attention we've spent months avoiding."

The workshop's careful secrecy, once a simple matter of discretion, had become something closer to an active liability — every additional patrol, every tightened checkpoint, every suspicious eye now turned toward the border represented one more chance that a search party asking the wrong questions might connect five ordinary-looking academics and craftsmen to whatever had actually happened at that stronghold.

"We can't act rashly," Seraphina said, the words clearly costing her as much as anyone else in the room. "If we go looking for him openly, we risk exposing everything he sacrificed himself to protect. If we do nothing, we risk—" She didn't finish that sentence either.

"He said he'd come back," Borgrim repeated, the same stubborn insistence he'd offered days earlier, though it carried less certainty now with each passing day of silence.

Ravencourt itself, meanwhile, settled slowly from premature celebration into a more complicated, uneasy mood as the reality of the standoff set in. The broadsheets that had once declared victory now printed more careful, measured analysis — the war wasn't over, whatever had happened to the Demon King notwithstanding, and the tightened border security made that plain to anyone paying attention.

"Refugees are having a harder time crossing now, too," Percival reported, returning from a supply run into the city with grim news of his own. "Not just military traffic — anyone trying to flee contested territory into safer ground is getting turned back or delayed at checkpoints that used to wave civilians through without much scrutiny. Whatever's got the Demon Kin spooked, it's made both sides more suspicious of everyone crossing that line."

"That's going to get worse before it gets better," Doran said, having remained at Ravencourt with his party rather than continuing toward the front, the entire group choosing to stay close given the uncertain situation. "Ilyana's been talking to some of the garrison captains. They're saying full lockdown could last months, until command's confident the retreat isn't a prelude to something worse."

Leandro's absence stretched into its second week, then its third, the workshop's careful secrecy becoming an increasingly heavy, isolating burden for the small circle who understood both the stakes and the silence.

"We keep working," Mirelle said finally, one evening, breaking a long stretch of nobody quite finding the words to address the mounting worry directly. "Whatever's happened to him, wherever he is — the project doesn't stop. Two dragon stones remain. The containment design is proven. If he's coming back, he'd want us continuing. And if—" her voice caught slightly, "—if something's happened to him, then finishing this properly is the only way any of it means something."

Borgrim nodded slowly, returning to his forge with grim, renewed focus. "For him, then. Until we know otherwise."

Outside the workshop, Ravencourt's tightened borders held firm, a continent's fragile hope caught in an uneasy standoff between two exhausted powers, and somewhere beyond that sealed border, alone and unaccounted for, the man who'd caused it all continued his own private, desperate struggle to find his way home.
