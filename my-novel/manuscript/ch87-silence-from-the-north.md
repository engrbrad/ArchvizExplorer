# Chapter 87 — Silence from the North

The letter arrived late, and wrong.

Doran's party had taken to writing regularly since Aldervine — brief, practical updates on their own slow progress toward the front, occasional requests for supplies Leandro was glad to provide. The rhythm had become comfortable enough that its absence, when three expected letters in a row simply didn't arrive, took longer than it should have to properly alarm anyone.

It was Ilyana's hand that finally broke the silence, though the letter wasn't from her.

"To whoever receives this — I write on behalf of the garrison at Corren's Watch. A party matching the description you provided in prior correspondence with our postmaster was found four days ago along the eastern ridge road, approximately two days' ride from our position. All five members deceased. Local assessment attributes the deaths to a Demon Kin ambush, though I confess certain details of the scene troubled our garrison commander enough that he asked me to include them here, in case they mean something to you that they did not mean to us."

Leandro read it twice before the words fully assembled themselves into meaning, and then a third time, some clinical, detached part of his mind cataloguing the details even as the rest of him went cold and distant in a way he recognized, distantly, as shock.

Doran. Ilyana. Percival. Bram — recovered, healed, finally reunited with his party only months earlier.

The letter continued, listing the "troubling details" the garrison commander had flagged: wounds inconsistent with typical Demon Kin claw or blade patterns; no tracks leading away from the scene despite the terrain's soft ground, which should have preserved a retreating force's passage clearly; valuables and weapons left entirely untouched, unusual for any scavenging force, demon or otherwise; and, most unsettling to the garrison commander, evidence that the party had been gathered together and killed in the same location, rather than cut down in the chaos of a genuine, moving battle.

This wasn't an ambush, Leandro thought, hands beginning to shake around the letter's edges. This was an execution.

He found Cassian first, unable to speak the news aloud without his voice breaking, simply handing over the letter and watching his friend's face cycle through the same disbelief, denial, and finally, devastating confirmation that his own had already suffered through alone.

"No," Cassian said, reading it a second time as though the words might rearrange themselves into something survivable. "No, that's — Bram just got his arm back. Ilyana was talking about settling down after the war, buying a small vineyard somewhere quiet. This doesn't—"

He couldn't finish the sentence. Neither of them could, for a long, silent stretch, the workshop's usual warmth suddenly feeling unbearably hollow around them.

Seraphina took the news worst of anyone, arriving from the front two days later once word reached her through slower channels, and the grief that broke through her usual composed, battle-hardened exterior was raw enough that Leandro found himself, despite his own devastation, needing to be the steady one, if only because someone had to be.

"They were investigating something," she said, once the first wave of grief had passed enough to allow speech. "Before this happened. Doran mentioned it in his last letter to me — something about strange financial patterns he'd stumbled onto near a garrison town, noble house correspondence that didn't add up to any legitimate war effort spending. He said he wanted to look into it properly before mentioning specifics, in case it turned out to be nothing."

Leandro felt something cold and furious settle into the pit of his stomach, the pieces of Threll's intelligence, Seraphina's own earlier suspicions about the invasion's timeline, and now this — five people he'd trusted, fought beside, genuinely loved as found family, executed and staged to look like an enemy's work — arranging themselves into a shape he didn't want to see, but couldn't any longer avoid.

"They found something," he said quietly. "Something real. Something worth killing five capable adventurers to bury."

"We don't know that for certain," Mirelle said carefully, though her own voice carried little real conviction against the theory.

"We know enough," Cassian said, voice flat and hard in a way Leandro had rarely heard from him. "Doran doesn't die to an ambush that leaves no tracks and no looting. Not that man. Not that party. Something found them first, and made sure they couldn't tell anyone what they'd learned."

The workshop fell into heavy, grief-stricken silence, five people processing the sudden, brutal loss of five more, and beneath the grief, in at least one heart in that room, something else was beginning to take root — cold, patient, and considerably more dangerous than anything Leandro had allowed himself to feel since watching an island disappear into the sea.
