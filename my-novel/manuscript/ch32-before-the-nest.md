# Chapter 32 — Before the Nest

They made camp at floor forty-five's edge for a full rest day before attempting forty-six, Doran insisting on it despite the party's evident eagerness to close the distance on a floor no one had ever confirmed clearing.

"We don't rush the wall everyone else has hit," Doran said. "We rest, we check every piece of gear twice, and we go in with clear heads."

It gave Leandro a quiet moment to actually sit with everything that had shifted over the past ten floors — Cassian's fighting noticeably sharper than it had been even a month ago, and Leandro found himself turning the change over honestly rather than simply accepting it at face value.

"You're better," Leandro said, watching Cassian oil the blade with careful, practiced attention. "But I don't think it's just training. Floor twenty nearly killed us both. Floor thirty-six, you handled something bigger without breaking a sweat. That's not a month of practice closing that gap alone."

Cassian didn't argue the observation, considering it seriously rather than brushing it off. "Some of it's the sword, honestly. It's been adapting to me longer now — reads my intent faster than it did at first, shifts before I've even fully committed to the motion. That's not nothing." He turned the blade once, watching firelight catch its edge. "But I think most of it is just... not fighting alone anymore. When it was just us, every fight felt like it had to go perfectly or one of us didn't walk away. Down here, with Bram holding a line and Percival covering openings, I can actually commit to a strike instead of half-holding back to cover my own retreat. Turns out I fight considerably better when I'm not also doing the job of three other people at once."

Leandro nodded slowly, the explanation settling into place with more honesty than a simple "I trained harder" ever could have. "That makes sense. Confidence and support changing what you're capable of executing, rather than some sudden leap in raw strength."

"Something like that," Cassian agreed. "I wouldn't call myself A-rank, whatever Percival said back on thirty-six. I'd call myself a B-rank finally fighting like one, instead of constantly bracing for the worst-case scenario. There's a difference."

"There is," Leandro agreed, quietly relieved by the distinction — proud of his friend's real growth, but glad it hadn't come from anything so implausible as raw talent multiplying overnight.

His own progress, by contrast, remained modest and honestly assessed — Yolanda's old measuring stick still the clearest framework he had for it. His earth affinity had grown perhaps half again what it had been when he'd first cracked it open on a collapsing canal wall, faster reach, slightly less exhaustion for comparable effort, but still solidly weak by any standard this dungeon's deeper floors demanded. Water remained his stronger suit, incrementally sharper through sheer repeated use, but nowhere close to Percival's raw output or even Ilyana's more modest but reliable elemental supplements.

"I'm still the weakest person on this team, magically," Leandro said, testing a small, compacted stone dome with visible effort, more proof of steady improvement than any real combat asset. "Better than I was. Not remotely close to what this floor's probably going to need from me directly."

"Nobody's asking you to carry this floor alone," Doran said, overhearing. "You've carried plenty already, just not with a sword. Trap awareness, environmental leverage, medical prep — that's exactly the kind of contribution that keeps six people alive long enough for the rest of us to do the parts we're actually built for."

"I know," Leandro said. "I just want to be honest about the shape of what I bring. It's not nothing, but it's not this floor's answer either."

Ferrin's antidote reserves got a final, careful inventory that evening — dosages checked, administration order rehearsed, contingencies discussed for every plausible poisoning scenario Leandro's research had surfaced. Percival ran his own quiet ritual of preparation, conserving magic deliberately rather than testing it, aware that whatever waited past this threshold would demand more sustained output than anything they'd faced yet. Bram sharpened gear that didn't strictly need sharpening, the particular ritual of a man settling his nerves through familiar motion. Ilyana and Doran reviewed the guild archive's scattered, incomplete accounts of forty-six one final time, searching for any detail previous failed expeditions might have left behind.

"Nobody's cleared this floor," Doran said finally, addressing the whole party as the fire burned low. "That means we don't know what's actually waiting past that door, beyond spiders and whatever venom they carry. I want everyone assuming worse than expected, and pleasantly surprised if it isn't."

"Understood," came the answer, quiet and unified, from around the fire.

Leandro glanced at Cassian one last time before settling in for what little rest anxious anticipation would allow — his friend's sword resting close at hand, expression calm in the specific, focused way Leandro had come to recognize as genuine readiness rather than forced confidence.

"Whatever happens tomorrow," Leandro said quietly, "we've come further than either of us thought possible a few months ago."

"We have," Cassian agreed, meeting his eyes. "Let's make sure we're still saying that tomorrow night."
