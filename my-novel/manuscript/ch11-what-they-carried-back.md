# Chapter 11 — What They Carried Back

The journey back to Millbrook took two days longer than the trip in, slowed by the grim weight of carrying Fessin and Corwin's bodies with the dignity Leandro had insisted on, wrapped carefully and carried in turns, no magic used to ease the burden beyond the practical necessity of Leandro's water occasionally clearing their path. Neither man spoke much along the way. Cassian's new sword rode sheathed at his hip, and Leandro's ring sat quiet and unassuming on his finger, and both of them had, by unspoken agreement, already settled on exactly how much of the truth the world outside that chamber was going to get.

Petra met them at Millbrook's edge, and whatever relief she might have felt at their safe return died the instant she saw what they carried.

"...The Halvern boy's friends made it out fine," she said quietly, staring at the wrapped forms. "I didn't think—"

"They made a mistake," Leandro said, and it cost him something to say it plainly rather than soften it. "A room deep in the cave was warded — protective magic around some old relics. They reached for something without waiting for us to understand what it was first. The ward judged them for it. There was nothing either of us could do to stop it once they'd already moved."

He didn't mention the ring. He didn't mention phasing through a lance of light half a second before it would have taken him too. Those were details that served no one, least of all a grieving village that needed the truth of what killed two of its guild-sent visitors, not the specifics of what Leandro himself had barely survived alongside them.

Petra was quiet for a long moment, then nodded slowly, the particular exhausted acceptance of someone who'd absorbed too much bad news this year to have much room left for shock. "I'll see to their rites properly. Guild will need telling too, formally. You'll have to report to Selvane."

"We know," Cassian said quietly. "We'll take them ourselves."

The report to the guild in Selvane took the better part of a day, delivered mostly by Leandro in the same flat, methodical tone he'd once used to explain sensor readings to panicking coworkers — the trap progression, the sealed chamber, the final warded room, the two surveyors' deaths laid out with clinical honesty and zero embellishment. He left out exactly two things: the ring, and the fact that something in him had flickered out of existence for half a second to save his own life. Everything else, he gave in full, including his own read on where the fault actually lay.

"I should have stopped them sooner," Leandro told Guildmaster Aldric Voss directly, an older man with the weathered patience of someone who'd heard a hundred variations of exactly this kind of report over a long career. "I identified the danger pattern too late to prevent it. That's on me, not just on their impatience."

Voss studied him for a long moment, the kind of look Leandro was starting to recognize as this world's particular flavor of reading a man's character rather than his words. "You went in with two guild surveyors and came back with one adventurer, one merchant, and a full account of everything that killed the two you lost. Most reports I get from sites like this are a lot shorter, Mister Valdez, usually because there's nobody left alive to write them."

"That's not the same as doing it right."

"No," Voss agreed. "But it's a good deal better than doing it wrong, which is what usually happens when I send a bigger team into a site like that. More bodies, more blood, and half the time we still don't get a clear account of what actually happened down there." He tapped the report on his desk. "You gave me a map of a dangerous site with confirmed hazards and a survivable path through it. That has value beyond the two lives it cost, grim as that is to say out loud."

Cassian, standing slightly behind Leandro, produced the sword next — laid across the desk with the specific reluctance of a man not entirely sure he wanted to give it up for inspection, even briefly.

Voss examined it with a practiced eye, testing its balance, running a thumb along the etched lines Leandro remembered pulsing gold the moment Cassian first touched it. His eyebrows rose slowly.

"This isn't ordinary work," Voss said. "Enchantment like this— " he turned it once more, then handed it back with obvious care, "—strength amplification woven directly into the metal, and unless I'm misreading these secondary runes, some manner of shape adaptation as well. I couldn't tell you the specifics without a proper enchanter's assessment, but I'd wager this blade does considerably more than what you're currently seeing from it."

"Shape adaptation," Cassian repeated slowly, looking down at his new sword with fresh interest.

"Some ancient smithwork could reconfigure a weapon's form to its wielder's need — a sword becoming a spear, a blade lengthening or shortening. I can't confirm that's what this does without deeper study, and I won't pretend to have the expertise to unlock it for you myself. That's beyond my rank." Voss looked between the two of them. "By guild law and precedent, items recovered from an unclaimed site belong to whoever survives the retrieval, provided the site's owner-kingdom doesn't stake a formal claim. Millbrook's too small to press ownership over ruins on its outskirts, and frankly, given what it cost you both to bring this out intact, I'm not inclined to argue the point even if they tried."

"And the other item?" Cassian asked, carefully vague, not naming the ring outright.

"You mentioned an unremarkable trinket in your report," Voss said, glancing at the paperwork. "Decorative, unclear function. I've no reason to doubt that, and no grounds to claim it even if I did."

Leandro kept his expression perfectly neutral, the same flat composure he'd used for six days underground, and said nothing further on the matter.

Voss set the report down fully then, folding his hands, and studied both of them with the weight of a man who'd spent decades learning to read truth from convenient fiction. "I'll be honest with you both, since you've been honest with me. Something about your account of those two deaths sits strange with me. Not wrong, necessarily — just incomplete in a way I can't put my finger on."

Leandro held perfectly still, the same discipline that had kept him alive through six days of traps holding now through a very different kind of pressure.

Voss let the silence sit a moment longer, then, unexpectedly, shook his head and let it go. "But I've read enough false reports in my time to know the shape of a lie, and this isn't one, not in the ways that matter. You didn't kill them for what they found. That much I'd stake my rank on." He exhaled, some of the formal weight leaving his posture. "And frankly, gentlemen — two dead is a mercy compared to what I've seen happen when a site like that gets cracked open by a large, undisciplined team. I've read the reports from Ravencourt's find. Eight months, and they lost eleven people before they finished clearing it properly. You gave me two names and a full map instead. I'll take that trade every time, however little I like saying so out loud."

He stood, extending a hand first to Cassian, then to Leandro. "Rest. Grieve, if you need to — sounds like you both earned that much, at least. The guild's in your debt for the map, whatever else came of it."

Outside, in the late afternoon light of Selvane's guild district, Cassian finally let out a breath he'd apparently been holding since Voss's comment about incomplete stories.

"He knows something's off," Cassian said quietly.

"He does," Leandro agreed. "He's also decided it doesn't matter enough to chase. That's not the same as being fooled. That's a man choosing which truths are worth the cost of pursuing."

"You trust that?"

Leandro considered the question seriously, the same way he considered any assessment of a person's reliability. "I trust that he's exactly as competent as his position requires him to be, and no more curious than his conscience demands. That's not blind trust. That's just an accurate read of the man in front of me." He glanced down at the ring, quiet and unassuming on his hand. "Which is the only kind of trust I plan on extending to anyone, for a while yet."

Cassian huffed something like a laugh, tired but genuine, and clapped a hand on Leandro's shoulder as they turned back toward the inn. "Fair enough, Valdez. Fair enough."
