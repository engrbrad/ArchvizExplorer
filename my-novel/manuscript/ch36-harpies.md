# Chapter 36 — Harpies

Floor forty-eight presented an entirely new problem — a wide, open chamber with a ceiling riddled with jagged outcroppings, home to a flock of harpies whose aerial mobility immediately negated much of the party's carefully developed ground-based coordination.

"This is going to be a problem," Doran said, watching the creatures wheel and shriek far above their reach, diving in fast, unpredictable strikes before retreating to the safety of height.

It proved, in practice, their most difficult floor yet.

Bram's shield, so reliable against ground threats, offered little protection against strikes coming from above and at odd angles. Ilyana's blade found nothing but air more often than not, the harpies' erratic flight patterns defeating her usual precise timing. Percival's wind magic, ironically, proved almost counterproductive — several early attempts simply aided the creatures' aerial maneuverability rather than hindering it, a lesson learned the hard way when a harpy used the very gust meant to ground it to instead execute an impossible mid-air pivot straight into a follow-up dive.

"Stop feeding them momentum!" Doran shouted, narrowly avoiding a diving strike aimed at his exposed shoulder. "We need something that actually disrupts them, not empowers the air around them!"

It was Leandro, watching the chaos with the same careful, cataloguing attention he applied to any failing system, who finally identified a workable answer. Water, unlike wind, offered the harpies no advantage at all — heavy, controlled streams aimed not to strike but to weigh down, soaking wings mid-flight and disrupting the delicate aerodynamics their attacks depended entirely upon.

"Aim for their wings, not their bodies!" Leandro called, already channeling a heavy stream toward the nearest diving harpy, watching it stagger mid-flight as sodden feathers suddenly failed to carry its weight properly.

The tactic, once identified, turned the fight considerably — Leandro's water grounding harpies just long enough for Bram or Ilyana to finish what gravity had started, Percival redirecting his own magic toward more controlled, precise strikes rather than the broad gusts that had briefly worked against them. It was still slow, still costly, three more grazing injuries claimed before the last harpy finally fell from the chamber's heights, but it was, unlike the giants, a genuine team solution rather than pure attrition.

"You found that fast," Percival said afterward, breathing hard, examining a shallow gash along his forearm with mild annoyance rather than real concern. "I nearly got someone killed feeding them wind before you figured out the counter."

"Wasn't fast," Leandro said honestly. "I just happened to have the one element in this fight that couldn't accidentally make things worse. Being lucky in your limitations is still luck, even when it looks like insight."

"I'll take useful luck over useless insight any day," Doran said, managing a tired smile despite the chamber's exhausting toll.
