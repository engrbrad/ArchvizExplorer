# Chapter 35 — Giants

Floor forty-seven's chamber was vast in a way none of the previous floors had prepared them for — ceiling lost somewhere in shadow far overhead, the space clearly built to accommodate something considerably larger than human scale.

The giant that met them confirmed it within moments — easily three times Bram's considerable height, skin like weathered stone, a crude club the size of a felled tree gripped in one massive hand.

"No poison here, at least," Percival said, some relief in his voice despite the obvious new danger. "Small mercies."

"Don't get comfortable," Doran warned, watching the creature's slow, deliberate advance. "Size like that means every hit counts double. We can't afford to trade blows evenly."

The fight unfolded as a careful, patient dance around raw physical power rather than speed or numbers — Bram's shield redirecting glancing blows rather than absorbing them outright, unwilling to test his own strength directly against something built on an entirely different scale. Ilyana and Cassian worked the giant's flanks, striking and retreating before the creature's ponderous reach could fully catch up, chipping away at its guard through sheer accumulated attrition rather than any single decisive blow.

Percival's magic, once again, proved the deciding factor — a concentrated blast aimed squarely at the back of the giant's knee, buckling its stance long enough for Bram to finally commit to a full, unguarded strike that toppled the creature entirely, the combined follow-up from Doran and Ilyana finishing what the fall had started.

It took nearly twenty exhausting minutes, considerably longer than any fight since the broodmother, and left the party breathing hard even for a single confirmed kill.

"There's more than one," Leandro observed grimly, noting fresh, heavy footprints leading deeper into the chamber. "This isn't a single guardian. This is a den."

They fought three more before finally clearing the floor properly, each fight demanding the same patient, attrition-based approach, no room for the reckless confidence smaller victories might have encouraged. By the time the chamber finally, truly quieted, the entire party was thoroughly spent, nerves frayed from the sheer physical scale of what they'd survived.

"That was exhausting in an entirely different way than the spiders," Ilyana said, sitting heavily against a wall. "Nothing subtle about it. Just relentless, patient grinding."

"I'll take patient grinding over poison any day," Bram said, flexing his shield arm carefully, testing for damage.
