# Chapter 26 — The Wall

The A-rank party parted ways with them at floor fifteen's boundary the following morning, Doran offering a firm handshake and genuine, unhurried advice before heading deeper toward whatever floor they'd been steadily working to reach.

"Fifteen's a fair marker for two on your own," Doran said. "Push further if you want to test yourselves — that's what this place is for. But know your limit before it finds you first. This dungeon doesn't care how clever or capable you are elsewhere. It only cares what you can survive on the floor you're standing on."

"We'll be careful," Leandro said.

"Everyone says that," Ilyana added, not unkindly, "right up until they're not." She offered a small, genuine smile despite the warning. "Good luck, though. Genuinely. Don't let my caution talk you out of finding your own answer."

They watched the party disappear down the deeper passage, and Cassian let out a breath once they'd gone. "Well. No pressure now, huh?"

"None at all," Leandro said dryly.

They pushed forward alone from there, and the difference was immediate and unforgiving.

Floor sixteen tested them with a pack of corrupted hounds whose coordinated hunting pattern nearly flanked Cassian before he adapted his sword's reach mid-fight, shifting it to the spear-length form just in time to hold the pack at bay while Leandro's water magic blinded and disoriented enough of them to break their formation. It worked, but it cost them both — Cassian bleeding from a shallow bite along his forearm, Leandro drained and shaking from the sustained magic output, a stark contrast to how effortlessly the A-rank party had handled comparable threats just floors above.

Floor eighteen was worse. A chamber of animated stone constructs, slow but nearly impervious to Cassian's blade regardless of its shifted form, forced them into a grinding, exhausting retreat-and-reposition strategy that took the better part of an hour to clear a single room the A-rank party would likely have dismantled in minutes.

"We're not equipped for this pace," Leandro said afterward, sitting heavily against a wall, arms trembling from exertion that had nothing to do with physical labor and everything to do with magic pushed well past its comfortable limit. "I'm burning through reserves faster than I can recover them. If we hit something like that construct room again, back to back, I don't know that I'll have enough left to matter."

"I'm not exactly running on full either," Cassian admitted, flexing his bandaged forearm with a wince. "B-rank on paper doesn't mean much when the terrain keeps demanding I fight three things at once with no backup covering my flank."

They made floor twenty by the narrowest possible margin — a chamber that greeted them with a monster unlike anything on the floors above, a hulking, chitinous horror that moved faster than its size suggested it should, and very nearly ended the expedition outright. Cassian took a glancing blow that sent him skidding across the stone floor hard enough to knock the wind fully from his lungs; Leandro's phase triggered involuntarily half a second before a follow-up strike would have finished what the first blow started, the familiar spike of pain behind his eyes dropping him to his knees the moment the danger passed, vision swimming, barely able to stand.

They survived. Barely. Retreating up through the floors they'd already cleared with the particular exhausted, adrenaline-shaking relief of people who understood, with total clarity now, exactly how close they'd come to not making it back at all.

They didn't speak much on the walk back to Brindlemark, both of them too drained and too honestly shaken to fill the silence with anything but recovery. It wasn't until they'd properly rested — two full days in a quiet corner of the inn, wounds tended, bodies allowed to actually recover rather than push forward on borrowed adrenaline — that Leandro finally addressed what they'd both been quietly turning over since floor twenty.

"We're not built for this alone," he said. "Not because we're weak, exactly — we made it further than I honestly expected, given what we're working with. But floor twenty nearly killed us both, and Doran's party treats that floor as barely worth mentioning. That's not a gap we close through effort alone. That's a gap that requires people whose actual strength complements what we're missing."

Cassian, still favoring his bandaged arm, nodded slowly. "I won't pretend my pride loves hearing that. But you're right. I'm strong enough to matter in a fair fight. That floor wasn't fair. Nothing down there past fifteen or so is fair for two people, no matter how capable either of us actually is."

"That's not failure," Leandro said, as much for himself as for Cassian. "That's data. We know our actual ceiling now, tested honestly rather than assumed. Twenty floors, two people, no outside support — that's genuinely further than I expected either of us to manage a month ago. I'm not ashamed of that number. I just understand, now, that going deeper means finding the right people to go deeper with."

Cassian managed a tired, genuine grin despite the exhaustion still written across his face. "Look at you. Turning a near-death experience into a productive planning session."

"Someone has to," Leandro said, allowing himself a small, matching smile. "Might as well be the one who's already used to filing incident reports."

They sat with that for a while — proud, in their own honest way, of twenty floors cleared through nothing but careful strategy and hard-earned competence, and equally clear-eyed about the wall they'd found waiting past it, a limit that no amount of individual growth alone would be enough to overcome.

"So," Cassian said eventually. "We build a team, then."

"We build a team," Leandro agreed.
