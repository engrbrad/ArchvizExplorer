# Chapter 6 — The Gathering Storm

*(Demon Kin POV — interlude, not on Leandro's personal timeline)*

Far north of Althea's fertile heartlands, past the mountain range humans called the Fangs and the Demon Kin called something older and harder to translate, the war council convened in a hall built from black stone that seemed to drink the torchlight rather than reflect it.

General Vharek Dromm stood at the head of a war table carved from a single slab of obsidian, watching the map's glowing markers shift under the light of suspended mana-crystals. He was not a large man, as Demon Kin went — his power had never come from size, but from the cold, patient calculation that had kept him alive through three failed human incursions and one civil purge that had claimed most of his rivals along the way.

"Althea's border patrols have thinned," said Serai, his second — younger, sharper-tongued, prone to impatience Vharek had long since stopped trying to correct out of her. "Half their standing garrison redirected south, to deal with bandit uprisings that are, I would remind everyone, entirely our doing."

A murmur of dark satisfaction moved through the assembled commanders. Vharek didn't join it. Satisfaction was a luxury for those who'd already won.

"And the ritual," Vharek said, not looking up from the map. "Confirm it."

Serai's smugness faltered, just slightly. "Confirmed. Our informants within the shrine network report the summoning failed to produce a combat-capable Hero. Whatever emerged from the circle was... unremarkable. The crown has suppressed the failure entirely — even most of their own nobility believe the ritual never completed."

That, Vharek thought, was the single most valuable piece of intelligence they'd acquired in a decade of patient war.

Every human kingdom built its deepest, most desperate contingency around the Hero ritual — a promise passed down through generations, that when the pressure became unbearable, salvation would be summoned from beyond the Veil to even impossible odds. It was a belief so foundational that entire military doctrines assumed its eventual activation. Reserves held back. Timelines calculated around "when the Hero arrives," not "if."

Althea had reached for that promise, and the promise had failed them. And they had chosen, out of pride or fear or simple political necessity, to bury the failure rather than admit it to their own people.

Which meant Althea's court now operated on a lie. Their nobility, their military command, quite possibly their own king, still carried the unspoken assumption that if the true crisis came, a Hero would answer. That assumption would shape every decision they made — how thin they stretched their defenses, how boldly they postured, how slowly they'd move to seek outside alliances they might otherwise have pursued already.

"They're planning around a foundation that doesn't exist," Vharek said quietly. "A palace built on a promise no one will keep."

"So we strike now," Serai said. "While they still believe help is coming."

"We strike," Vharek agreed, "when they believe it least matters that help is not." He traced a clawed finger along the map, from the northern passes down toward the gleaming marker that denoted Althea's capital. "Not a border skirmish. Not another bandit proxy. When we move against Althea, we move against the palace itself — swift, total, before any neighboring kingdom can mobilize support, and before Althea's court can quietly attempt a second ritual to correct their first mistake."

Silence settled over the table. Even Serai, for once, said nothing.

"How long," she finally asked, "before we're ready?"

Vharek considered the map a moment longer — the thinned garrisons, the false confidence, the quiet lie sitting at the heart of Althea's court like a crack in a foundation no one had bothered to inspect.

"Months," he said. "Perhaps a year. We are patient, and they are comfortable. Comfort makes people slow." He looked up, meeting the eyes of every commander in the hall in turn. "When we come, it will not be a battle they can recover from. It will be the day the crown's silence becomes their undoing."

---

Outside, unseen and unheard by anyone in that black stone hall, a nuclear engineer with a low-Novice affinity for water was three weeks into learning how to purify a well for a farming village that had never had clean water before, entirely unaware that the decision he'd made — walking away from a palace position to see the world with his own eyes — had already put him a continent's width away from a fire that hadn't yet been lit.
