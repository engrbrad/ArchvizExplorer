# Chapter 86 — Building Aldervine

The team arrived a week later, considerably less discreet than Leandro's solo scouting trip had been, though still modest by design — no grand announcement, no fanfare, simply a small group of skilled people and a wagon of tools and supplies rolling into a town that had long since stopped expecting anyone to actually follow through on promises of help.

"You're the fellow from before," Petrus said, eyeing the wagon and its occupants with cautious surprise. "Didn't expect you back so soon. Or with company."

"I said I'd help if the assessment supported it," Leandro said, dropping the disguise now that the scouting phase had passed — there was little point in anonymity once the actual work began drawing attention regardless. "It did. This is my team."

Introductions were brief, practical, and the town's wary skepticism didn't fully dissolve until the actual work began and word spread, the way it always did, through demonstrated results rather than promises.

Borgrim took the well first, the same patient, exacting standard he brought to every piece of precision work applied now to a problem considerably simpler than crystal containment, but no less deserving of his full attention.

"This pulley's older than my grandfather's forge," he muttered, examining the mechanism with a craftsman's mix of disdain and professional interest. "No wonder it groans like a dying beast."

He had it replaced within four days, working alongside two of Aldervine's own young men who watched his technique with the same rapt attention Dagen had once shown Leandro back in Millbrook — knowledge changing hands, quietly, the way it always seemed to in these small, unglamorous projects.

Mirelle, less accustomed to fieldwork than laboratory precision, found her own unexpected niche examining the town's soil composition, identifying exactly why the northern fields' drainage had failed so consistently.

"Your fields aren't just poorly graded," she told Petrus, spreading soil samples across a makeshift table. "The composition itself doesn't drain well to begin with — too much clay content, not enough organic matter to break it up properly. Regrading helps, but you'll want to work compost and ash into these specific plots over the next two seasons if you want the improvement to actually last."

"You're not just an engineer's assistant, are you," Petrus said, something like genuine respect entering his voice for the first time.

"I'm a crystal researcher, mostly," Mirelle admitted, with a faint, wry smile. "But dirt has patterns too, if you know how to look at them properly."

Cassian, for his part, handled the bridge — not the structural repair itself, which Leandro and Borgrim managed together, but the delicate work of coordinating labor, materials, and the particular local politics of a small town where everyone had an opinion about how things ought to be done.

Leandro noticed, watching his friend move through Aldervine's streets with easy, practiced charm, how carefully Cassian avoided the northern road that led toward the old Ashford manor's ruins — never mentioning it, never glancing toward it even when others did, a small, deliberate omission that confirmed, without a single word exchanged, everything Petrus's story had already suggested.

He said nothing. He simply noted it, filed it away with the same patient care as everything else, and let his friend carry the weight of that old, unspoken history at whatever pace he needed.

Seraphina, present for the project's second week once her own duties allowed brief leave, brought a different kind of contribution — not infrastructure, but the particular, hard-won authority of a recognized war hero, lending her name and visible presence to reassure Aldervine's understandably skeptical population that this reconstruction effort carried real, credible backing rather than empty promise.

"People trust a face they've heard of," she said, when Leandro thanked her for the visit. "Even if what they've heard is mostly exaggerated broadsheet nonsense. Might as well put that to good use."

The bridge, once repaired, held with a solidity that made Aldervine's northern farmers weep openly with relief, no longer needing to risk the dangerous, failing crossing every harvest season. The fields, regraded and treated according to Mirelle's careful specifications, showed their first proper improvement within a single growing season, yields climbing back toward something resembling the town's earlier, more prosperous years. The well ran smooth and reliable, exactly as Millbrook's once had, drawing water with a fraction of the effort it had previously demanded.

By the project's end, six weeks after they'd first arrived, Aldervine had transformed from a town quietly, invisibly failing into one with genuine, visible hope for its own continued survival — not dramatically, not with any fanfare the wider war-torn continent would ever notice or record, but completely, and permanently, in exactly the unglamorous way Leandro had always found most meaningful.

"You didn't have to do any of this," Petrus told him, on their final evening in town, something genuinely moved beneath his usual gruff, weathered manner. "Whatever you and your people actually are, whatever brought you here — Aldervine won't forget it."

"That's not why we did it," Leandro said honestly. "But I appreciate you saying so regardless."

He glanced toward Cassian, standing a short distance away, gaze fixed, briefly and unguardedly, on the distant hills where an old, ruined manor sat waiting for a homecoming its former heir clearly wasn't ready to make.

Someday, Leandro thought, watching his friend's carefully composed expression. Whenever you're ready. I'll be here when you are.
