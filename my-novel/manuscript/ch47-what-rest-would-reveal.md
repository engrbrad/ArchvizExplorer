# Chapter 47 — What Rest Would Reveal

The camp behind the pass settled into the particular quiet of aftermath — wounded tended, dead accounted for, survivors moving through the motions of recovery with the mechanical efficiency of people who'd done this too many times to feel it properly anymore.

Seraphina slept for nearly a full day once the adrenaline finally released its hold on her, and Leandro used the time productively, moving through the camp the way he'd moved through Millbrook and Ostwick before it — noting what was broken, what could be fixed with the materials at hand, quietly reinforcing a section of barricade that had nearly given way during the fight, redirecting a contaminated runoff stream away from the camp's drinking water source before it could sicken anyone already weakened by injury.

"You don't stop, do you," Cassian observed, watching him work.

"Stopping doesn't fix anything," Leandro said, testing the reinforced barricade's stability with careful hands. "I'll rest when there's nothing useful left to do."

"That's going to be never, at this rate."

Leandro didn't have a good answer for that, so he didn't offer one.

Seraphina found him again the following evening, considerably more herself — color returned to her face, the particular sharp alertness he remembered from Ostwick's camps back in full force.

"I tested it properly," she said, without preamble, sitting across the small fire from him and holding up the bracelet still worn on her wrist. "Fully rested. Controlled conditions, away from the pass." She paused, something almost unsettled behind her usual confidence. "Yesterday wasn't remotely its full potential, Valdez. I was running on fumes when you gave it to me. What I felt just now, testing it properly—" She shook her head slowly. "I don't have adequate words for it yet. I'm going to need days to actually understand the scale of what I'm holding."

"How much stronger?" Leandro asked, genuinely curious now, filing the information away with careful attention.

"Enough that I'm almost afraid to use it carelessly," she admitted. "Enough that I understand now why you said your own affinity couldn't make use of it properly. This isn't a modest boost. This is a different category of output entirely." She studied him for a long moment. "You gave this away without knowing that. Without testing it yourself under real conditions."

"I tested what I needed to know," Leandro said. "That it worked, and that my own magic couldn't make it matter the way yours clearly can. The rest was information I didn't need in order to make the right decision."

She was quiet for a while after that, watching the fire, something clearly turning over behind her sharp, exhausted eyes. "You keep doing this," she said eventually. "Giving away exactly what matters most, to whoever needs it more than you do. First the camp work in Ostwick. Now this." She looked up. "Why?"

Leandro considered the question seriously, the way he considered most things. "Because I've spent months watching what happens when people who could have helped chose not to, or couldn't figure out how in time," he said slowly. "Voss chose to hold a street he didn't have to hold, and thousands of people are alive because of it. You've been holding this pass for months, and I don't think anyone's fully appreciated what that's actually cost you to sustain." He paused. "I have things I can give. Knowledge, a strange ring nobody else can use anyway, a bracelet that means nothing in my hands and everything in yours. Keeping any of that for its own sake, when I could see exactly what it would cost not to share it, would have felt like exactly the kind of failure I've spent this whole war watching other people make."

Seraphina studied him for a long moment, something unreadable behind her exhaustion. "That's a heavy way to live, Valdez."

"Probably," he admitted. "I haven't found a lighter way that still lets me sleep at night."
