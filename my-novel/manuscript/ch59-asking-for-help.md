# Chapter 59 — Asking for Help

Leandro raised the idea with Cassian the night before their planned departure, turning over a problem that had been quietly nagging at him since Seraphina first laid out the vault's location.

"Selvane's ruins," he said, staring at the rough map they'd assembled from her contacts. "Contested, unstable, actively patrolled by Demon Kin scavengers. Just the two of us went from barely surviving floor twenty of a dungeon to needing five other people's help clearing floor fifty. This is considerably more dangerous than a dungeon with predictable rules."

"You're thinking we call in reinforcements," Cassian said, already following the logic.

"I'm thinking we'd be reckless not to." Leandro considered the map a moment longer. "Doran's party. They're already moving toward the front, by their own choice, looking for exactly the kind of purpose this represents. And we already trust them, which matters more right now than raw strength alone."

"What do we tell them?" Cassian asked. "The truth?"

Leandro was quiet for a long moment, weighing that carefully. "Not the whole truth. Not yet. Too many people knowing what we're actually building, before it's controlled enough to be safe, is its own kind of danger." He looked up. "But I don't want to lie to people who've already bled alongside us either. I'll tell them we're searching for something that could genuinely change the war's outcome. That's true, without revealing the specifics of what it actually is."

"And if they ask what, exactly?"

"Then I ask for their trust the same way I asked for Voss's, back when I chose not to explain my phasing," Leandro said. "I've earned a little of that, I think, across everything we've survived together. I'd rather spend it carefully than lie outright."

They found Doran's party three days' hard ride from Ravencourt's territory, still making their own steady way toward the front, and the reunion carried the easy warmth of people who'd genuinely missed each other's company despite the short time apart.

"Valdez. Cassian." Doran clasped both their hands in turn, genuine welcome in his weathered face. "Didn't expect to see either of you again this soon. Thought you were chasing down the Ember Blade's location."

"We found her," Leandro said. "That's part of why we're here now."

He laid out the situation carefully, choosing his words with the same deliberate precision he'd used on Guildmaster Voss's report months earlier — honest in substance, careful in specifics. "I've been working on something, with people I trust, that could genuinely shift the war's momentum. I won't lie to you about what it fully is — not because I don't trust you, but because it's dangerous enough that fewer people knowing the details is safer for everyone involved, including you. What I can tell you is that finishing it properly requires something specific, something I believe survived in Selvane's ruins — likely still sitting in what's left of the palace vault."

Ilyana's eyebrows rose. "You want to go into Selvane's ruins. Looking for something in the vault. That's not a small ask, Valdez."

"I know," Leandro said. "I wouldn't ask if I thought two of us could manage it alone. The ruins are contested, actively patrolled, and I have no illusions about how dangerous that makes the search."

Percival studied him carefully, the same sharp attention he'd once turned on Cassian's transformed sword. "Something that could change the war's outcome," he repeated slowly. "You're being deliberately vague."

"I am," Leandro admitted. "For good reason. I'd rather you trust that reason than press me for details I'm not ready to share yet."

It was Seraphina, who'd traveled with them partway before returning to her own post, who spoke up before the silence stretched too long. "I've seen what he's built already," she said quietly, the weight of firsthand witness carrying real authority in her voice. "I can't tell you the specifics either — that's his choice to make, not mine. But I can tell you, plainly, that what I've seen convinces me this is worth the risk. If he says finishing it matters this much, I believe him. I wouldn't stake my own judgment on it otherwise."

Doran studied Seraphina for a long moment — her reputation alone carrying weight even among people who'd never fought beside her directly — and then looked back to Leandro with something settling into resolved decision behind his eyes.

"That's good enough for me," Doran said finally. "You've never given us reason to doubt your judgment, Valdez, not once across everything we've survived together. If the Ember Blade herself is willing to stake her word on it without knowing the full specifics, I'm willing to trust that too."

"Same," Ilyana said, and Bram nodded his own quiet agreement.

Percival hesitated a moment longer, clearly still turning the vagueness over, before finally relenting. "I don't love not knowing what we're actually risking ourselves for. But I trust the people vouching for it more than I distrust the mystery." He managed a faint, wry smile. "Besides. After that broodmother and those golems, I don't think there's much left down here that scares me more than curiosity does."

"Thank you," Leandro said, genuine relief settling into his chest. "All of you. I don't take this lightly."

"We know," Doran said simply. "That's rather the point of trusting you in the first place."

They set out together the following morning, six people now bound for a fallen capital's dangerous ruins in search of something only two of them fully understood the true purpose of — and Leandro found himself, watching the familiar, capable party fall into their easy traveling rhythm around him, grateful beyond easy expression for exactly the kind of trust that Voss's own example had first taught him to value so completely.
