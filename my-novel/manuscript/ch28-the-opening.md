# Chapter 28 — The Opening

The priest Doran's party hired was, by Brindlemark's standards, extraordinarily expensive — a specialist contracted from Ravencourt's academy circuit, the kind of healer whose fees alone could fund a modest expedition outright. Money well spent, apparently; within two days, Percival was walking the guild hall's upper floor with nothing worse than a lingering stiffness in his shoulder, ribs fully knit, the broken bones and internal bruising erased as though the fall had never happened.

"Remarkable work," Leandro said, genuinely impressed, when Cassian relayed the details secondhand.

"Remarkably expensive work," Cassian corrected. "That's the kind of price tag that makes even a successful A-rank party wince a little, checking their coin purse afterward."

It was, in fact, that very cost — combined with the practical matter of a destroyed storage case still needing full replacement before any serious expedition could resume — that brought Doran to their table in the guild hall's common room three days later, without much preamble.

"You've been trying to build a team," Doran said, settling into the seat across from them uninvited but not unwelcome. "Heard about it through the usual channels. No shortage of people in this town happy to gossip about who's struggling to find company."

"Word travels fast," Leandro said, wry rather than offended.

"It does." Doran's quiet, unassuming manner didn't shift, though something in his tone carried a proposal now rather than idle conversation. "We're grounded for at least another two weeks — Percival's healed, but we don't push back into that dungeon without a full resupply, and frankly, after what happened, I'd rather not rush the preparation this time. Which leaves us with time, and leaves you two with an opening, if you're interested."

"Interested in what, specifically?" Cassian asked, leaning forward.

"Joining us for the next expedition. Properly, this time — not tagging along the shallow floors while we do the real work." Doran's gaze settled on Leandro with particular attention. "That trick with the dust on floor twelve wasn't nothing. Neither was managing food supply for six people without anyone noticing the strain on your own gear. I don't know exactly what you're carrying, and I'm not asking. But it's useful, and useful is what this team needs right now, especially with Percival's storage gone and a fresh case still needing to prove itself durable before we trust it the way we trusted the old one."

Leandro exchanged a glance with Cassian, the same silent communication they'd built over months of shared decisions passing between them in an instant.

"We'd need to prepare properly," Leandro said carefully. "Not just show up. If we're going deeper than fifteen this time — and I assume that's the intention — I want us stocked for what's actually down there. Floor forty-six's poison alone would need dedicated antidote reserves, assuming we're even attempting to push that far this trip."

"We're not," Doran said. "Not yet. Realistic target's floor twenty-five, maybe thirty if things go unusually well. But planning for contingencies beyond the immediate goal isn't unreasonable, given what just happened to Percival on a floor we thought we understood." He extended a hand. "Interested?"

Leandro shook it without hesitation. "Interested."

The two weeks that followed were, for Leandro, some of the most satisfying he'd spent since arriving in this world — not because of any single achievement, but because preparation, real methodical preparation, was a language he'd spoken fluently his entire adult life, and Brindlemark's dungeon gave him a genuinely worthy problem to apply it to.

He spent the first days simply gathering information — visiting the guild archive to review every recorded account of floors sixteen through thirty that previous expeditions had bothered documenting, cross-referencing monster types, environmental hazards, structural quirks like the one that had nearly cost Percival his life. He compiled a working list, methodical and thorough, of exactly what each floor's known dangers demanded in terms of supply.

Antidotes came first, and proved the most involved — floor forty-six's poisonous spiders were well beyond their planned depth this trip, but Leandro insisted on stocking a baseline regardless, reasoning that venom-resistant preparation research, once started, was worth having on hand for future expeditions rather than scrambling to source it later under time pressure. He spent two full days with Brindlemark's resident apothecary, a sharp, no-nonsense woman named Ferrin who warmed to his detailed, specific questions about toxin composition and treatment thresholds in a way she clearly didn't extend to most adventurers who wandered in asking for "the strong stuff."

"You ask questions like someone who actually wants to understand the poison, not just survive it," Ferrin observed, mixing a fresh batch of general-purpose antivenom while Leandro took careful notes on dosage and administration timing.

"Understanding it means I don't have to guess under pressure," Leandro said. "I'd rather know exactly what I'm treating than hope the first thing I grab works well enough."

Food came next, and here his storage ring did quiet, invisible work that the rest of the party never fully questioned — fresh bread, preserved meats, dried vegetables, and enough clean water reserves to outlast any reasonable expedition length, all maintained in the same perfect, timeless preservation he'd tested months earlier in Selvane, though he packaged and presented it to the group in modest, ordinary-looking portions that gave no hint of the actual scale behind them.

Cassian, meanwhile, handled equipment — new rope rated for weights well beyond anything they'd need, replacement components for Percival's storage case, a proper set of climbing gear given the structural instability that had already proven itself a genuine hazard. He moved through Brindlemark's markets with the easy, practiced confidence of a man who knew exactly which merchants to trust and which to haggle down without insult, and Leandro found himself, more than once, quietly grateful for exactly how capable his friend's less flashy skills actually were.

By the end of the two weeks, their combined preparation had assembled a supply stock that made even Doran's experienced party visibly impressed — organized, redundant where redundancy mattered, and thorough in a way that spoke to genuine care rather than simple thoroughness for its own sake.

"You could run logistics for a standing army with this level of preparation," Percival said, testing the weight and balance of his replacement storage case with careful, healed hands. "Most parties our rank still wing half of this."

"Most problems are easier to survive if you've already thought about them before they happen," Leandro said simply. "I'd rather spend two weeks preparing than one bad afternoon improvising."

Doran, watching the assembled supplies with quiet approval, allowed himself something close to a genuine smile. "Welcome to the team properly, then, both of you. Let's see what twenty-five floors looks like with six of us instead of four."
