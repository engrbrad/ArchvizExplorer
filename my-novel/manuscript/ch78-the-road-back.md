# Chapter 78 — The Road Back

The cart ride to the market town near Ravencourt's outskirts took two unremarkable days, jostling along rutted roads behind a pair of tired draft horses while the driver, an incurious older farmer named Hobb, filled the silence with idle complaints about checkpoint delays and grain prices that Leandro absorbed gratefully as further confirmation of just how much the war's disruption had settled into ordinary life's background noise.

They passed through two checkpoints along the way, both considerably less thorough than Maribel's warning had led him to fear — a cursory glance at Hobb's cargo manifest, a bored guard's brief once-over of Leandro's unremarkable, farmhand appearance, and they were waved through both times with barely a delay. Whatever heightened tension gripped the border regions closer to contested territory clearly hadn't fully propagated this far into Ravencourt's secured interior.

He parted ways with Hobb at the market town's edge, offering genuine thanks alongside his last few coins, and covered the final stretch to Ravencourt proper on foot, the academy city's familiar spires and walls rising into view with a sight that made something tight in his chest finally, cautiously begin to loosen.

Almost there, he thought, exhaustion and anticipation warring in equal measure. Almost home.

He reached the workshop's outer district in the late afternoon, six weeks and some days since he'd launched into the night sky above the Fangs, and found the familiar building looking exactly as he'd left it — modest, unassuming, giving no outward hint of the small, desperate hope its occupants had been holding onto through weeks of anxious silence.

He knocked, too exhausted for anything more dramatic than the simple, ordinary gesture, and heard footsteps approach from within.

Cassian opened the door.

For a long, frozen moment, neither of them said anything at all, Cassian's face cycling through disbelief, shock, and something raw and overwhelmed that Leandro had rarely seen from his composed, easy-mannered friend.

"You—" Cassian's voice cracked entirely, and whatever he'd intended to say dissolved into a rough, wordless sound as he pulled Leandro into an embrace fierce enough to aggravate his still-healing ribs, though Leandro found he didn't particularly mind the pain.

"I told you I'd come back," Leandro managed, his own voice unsteady in a way he rarely allowed.

"Six weeks," Cassian said, pulling back just enough to properly look at him, taking in the gaunt, weathered condition Leandro knew he must present after everything the journey had cost him. "Six weeks of nothing, Valdez. We didn't know if—" He stopped himself, shaking his head, and simply pulled Leandro through the doorway instead. "Everyone. Everyone needs to see this."

The workshop's interior erupted into a chaos of relief the moment the others registered who'd just walked through the door — Mirelle abandoning her instruments mid-calculation, Borgrim's gruff composure cracking entirely as he crossed the room in three long strides, Seraphina, present for one of her periodic visits from the front, simply staring for a long moment before crossing to embrace him with fierce, undisguised relief.

"We thought—" Mirelle started, then stopped, unable to finish the sentence any more than Cassian had managed.

"I know what you thought," Leandro said quietly. "I'm sorry. I couldn't send word. The border's sealed tighter than any of us anticipated, and I spent most of the journey simply trying to stay alive and unnoticed."

"You're hurt," Seraphina said, professional concern briefly overriding her relief as she took in his condition with a healer's practiced eye. "Properly hurt, not just travel-worn."

"Cracked ribs, mostly healed now. A sprained ankle that's given me considerable trouble. Exhaustion beyond anything I've felt since arriving in this world." He managed a tired, genuine smile despite it all. "But alive. That's the important part."

Borgrim gripped his shoulder hard, the dwarf's usual gruff reserve entirely abandoned. "We heard about the stronghold. The King. Whatever happened up there—"

"It worked," Leandro confirmed quietly. "Exactly as we hoped, though I won't pretend the delivery went as smoothly as the design itself. I'll tell you everything. All of it. But I think I need to sit down first, and possibly eat something that isn't foraged mountain plants before I manage a proper account."

That earned a tired, relieved laugh from the whole room, the accumulated weeks of anxious uncertainty finally, properly breaking into something like genuine celebration.

He told them everything that evening, once food and rest had restored enough of his strength to manage a coherent account — the flight, the fortress, the devastating, successful detonation, the desperate walk through hostile territory, the demon named Skarn and the uncomfortable, complicating truth about the invasion's actual origins that had settled uneasily into his understanding of the entire war.

"Famine," Seraphina said slowly, absorbing that particular revelation with visible difficulty. "That's not the narrative anyone on our side has ever considered."

"It doesn't excuse what's been done," Leandro said. "Selvane. Everyone this war has killed on both sides. But I don't think we can build toward anything resembling a real ending without understanding that the enemy isn't purely, simply evil. Desperate, yes. Capable of atrocity, certainly. But desperate people make different choices than purely malicious ones, if anyone's ever given them the opportunity to choose differently."

The room absorbed that in thoughtful, complicated silence, the weight of everything he'd learned settling alongside the relief of his safe return.

"One thing at a time," Cassian said finally, breaking the heavy quiet with the same steady practicality he'd offered Leandro across every crisis they'd weathered together. "You're back. You're alive. The weapon works, and it's already changed the shape of this war more than anything else that's happened since Selvane fell. Whatever comes next — the demons' true motives, the dragon stone still waiting in your ring, whatever we do with everything we've learned — that's tomorrow's problem."

"Tonight," Borgrim added gruffly, "we're just glad you're home."

Leandro looked around the room — at Cassian's relieved, steady presence, at Mirelle's fierce intelligence, at Borgrim's gruff warmth, at Seraphina's fierce, protective care — and felt, for the first time since watching an island vanish beneath an uncontrolled blast so many months ago, something that had been missing throughout the entire exhausting, dangerous journey home.

Not relief alone. Something closer to belonging.

"Home," he echoed quietly. "Yes. I think that's exactly what this is."
