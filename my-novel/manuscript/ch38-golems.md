# Chapter 38 — Golems

Floor fifty greeted them with a stillness that felt, immediately, different from every floor before it — no immediate threat visible, just a vast chamber of towering stone constructs standing motionless around its perimeter, easily as tall as the giants they'd faced two floors prior, but silent, unmoving, watching without watching.

"Golems," Percival said quietly. "Guild records mention them at rumored deeper floors. Nobody's confirmed actually reaching this far before."

"They're not moving," Ilyana said, equally cautious. "Are they even active?"

The answer came the moment Bram, testing the theory with careful, deliberate steps toward the chamber's center, crossed some invisible threshold — the nearest golem's eyes flared with sudden, cold light, and its massive stone form ground to motion with a deep, resonant grinding sound that echoed through the entire chamber.

"They're active now," Bram said, already raising his shield.

The fight that followed tested every lesson the party had gathered across the last four floors combined — the golems' raw physical resilience demanded the same patient attrition as the giants, their sheer number and simultaneous activation requiring the same careful, coordinated positioning the harpies had forced them to develop, and scattered structural weaknesses in the chamber's ancient floor meant Leandro's trap-awareness from floor forty-nine remained just as critical here as it had been there.

Percival's magic strained to find purchase against stone that simply absorbed most conventional force, forcing the party into a grinding, methodical fight focused on precise joint-strikes and accumulated damage rather than any single decisive blow. Cassian's blade, shifted to its heaviest, most concussive form, proved unexpectedly effective against golem joints in a way its lighter forms hadn't managed against the giants' softer, more vulnerable flesh.

"Aim for the connections, not the mass!" Doran called, light magic flaring along his repaired blade as he demonstrated the technique himself, a precise strike disabling one golem's arm entirely at the shoulder joint.

It took the better part of two hours, the longest single fight the party had endured since arriving at this depth, five golems in total before the chamber finally, fully quieted once more, stone fragments scattered across a floor that had tested every single member of the party's accumulated growth in equal measure.

They collapsed into their next rest point with the particular, bone-deep exhaustion of people who'd genuinely earned every inch of the depth behind them.

"Fifty floors," Leandro said quietly, staring up at nothing in particular, too tired for much more than simple, honest disbelief. "I don't think either of us imagined this, back on floor twenty."

"No," Cassian agreed, equally spent. "But I'm glad we found out what came after."
