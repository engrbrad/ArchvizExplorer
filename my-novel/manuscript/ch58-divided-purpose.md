# Chapter 58 — Divided Purpose

They reconvened at the workshop two days later, the weight of the island's destruction still hanging over every conversation, and worked through the division of labor with the same careful, deliberate planning Leandro brought to everything.

"We can't all chase dragon stones," Leandro said, laying out the practical reality plainly. "Selvane's ruins are dangerous, contested territory, and I won't risk this entire team on a search that might turn up nothing. But we also can't simply sit still with what we've already built — the current design works, in the sense that it's proven catastrophically effective. It doesn't work in the sense that it's controllable, proportional, or safe enough for anyone but me to even consider testing further."

"So we split the work," Borgrim said, already following the logic. "Some of us keep refining what we've got. Others go looking for something better to refine it with."

"That's the plan," Leandro confirmed. "Borgrim, Mirelle — I need you here, continuing to work the containment problem. If dragon stones exist and we find them, I want a housing and synchronization system ready that can actually handle a purer, more stable core without the runaway cascade we just witnessed. That's not a small task on its own."

"It's the harder half, honestly," Mirelle said, though there was no complaint in it, just clinical assessment. "Redesigning containment for a fuel source we haven't even confirmed exists yet, based on theoretical density estimates alone."

"I trust you to manage the theory," Leandro said. "Cassian and I will handle the search itself."

Seraphina, listening quietly until this point, shook her head slowly. "Not without me contributing something useful, at least. I can't leave my post here — the pass still needs holding, and I won't abandon that duty even for this. But I have connections at the academy, old contacts from before the war who might know more about the crown's actual salvage efforts after Selvane fell. If anyone catalogued what survived the palace's vaults before the ruins became contested territory, it'll be through channels I can access faster than you could on your own."

"That would help considerably," Leandro said, genuinely grateful. "Anything narrowing the search saves time we don't have to waste."

She spent the following two days working her contacts, and returned to the workshop with a folder of carefully gathered information — old inventory records, secondhand accounts from surviving palace staff who'd escaped the fall, rumors from scavengers who'd already picked through parts of the ruins looking for less dangerous, more immediately sellable loot.

"The crown's vault held exactly three dragon stones, according to the most reliable inventory I could find," Seraphina said, spreading the documents across the workshop table. "Ceremonial relics, mostly — passed down through generations of Althea's royal line, valued more for prestige than practical use, since nobody currently living understood how to properly draw on them. If they weren't destroyed outright when the palace fell, they're most likely still somewhere in the vault's wreckage, assuming looters haven't already found and moved them."

"Assuming," Cassian echoed grimly. "That's doing a lot of work in that sentence."

"It's the best information available," Seraphina said. "The ruins are still too dangerous for the crown's own remaining forces to properly secure — too many Demon Kin patrols still working the area, drawn by the same salvage opportunities everyone else wants. Whatever's left in that vault has probably sat undisturbed specifically because it's too dangerous to reach casually."

Leandro studied the documents carefully, cross-referencing the vault's described location against his own memory of Selvane's palace layout from the brief time he'd spent there months ago. "Then that's where we start."

They departed three days later, the workshop settling into its divided purpose — Borgrim and Mirelle remaining behind with the full weight of the containment redesign, Leandro and Cassian preparing for a journey back toward the one place in this world Leandro had never expected, or particularly wanted, to return to.

"You'll be careful," Seraphina said, seeing them off at the academy's edge, something genuine and worried beneath her usual composed exterior. "Selvane's ruins aren't a dungeon floor with predictable rules. They're contested territory, unstable, full of people and things with every reason to want you dead."

"We'll be careful," Leandro said. "I have every intention of coming back to finish what we've started here."

"See that you do," she said, and the farewell carried more weight than either of them fully voiced aloud.

Borgrim clasped Leandro's arm firmly before they departed, the dwarf's usual gruff manner softened by something more sincere. "Find what you're looking for. I'll have a housing ready that can actually hold it properly, whenever you manage to bring one back."

"I'm counting on it," Leandro said.

Mirelle, characteristically, offered no sentiment at all beyond a final, pointed instruction. "Document everything about the stones themselves, if you find them — size, color, any visible internal structure you can observe without damaging them. I'll need every detail to properly calculate the containment specifications before you even get back."

"Understood," Leandro said, managing a tired smile despite the weight of what lay ahead.

He and Cassian set out toward Selvane's ruins the following morning, the wagon loaded light and fast rather than heavy with supplies, both of them acutely aware that whatever waited in that fallen capital's wreckage would demand speed, caution, and considerably more luck than either of them had any right to expect.

"Back to where this all started," Cassian said, watching the road stretch out ahead of them. "Feels strange, doesn't it? Everything that's happened since that summoning circle, and we're circling back to the same city."

"Nothing about this feels like circling back," Leandro said quietly, watching the horizon with the same careful, resolved focus he'd carried since the island's destruction had settled fully into his understanding. "It feels like finally arriving somewhere I should have gone months ago."
