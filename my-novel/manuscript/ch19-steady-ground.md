# Chapter 19 — Steady Ground

The crisis in Ostwick didn't end so much as it gradually stopped being a crisis and became simply the new shape of the city — refugee camps slowly transitioning into semi-permanent districts, the consortium formalizing food and housing allocations that had started as improvised triage, Baldric's party and the other visiting adventurers rotating out as monster activity along the routes back to Selvane's ruins finally justified proper patrols instead of pure crowd management.

By the twelfth week, Ostwick had absorbed its swollen population into something resembling a functioning, if permanently altered, city. Leandro's drainage systems held through the season's first real storm without a single flooded shelter, a fact Yara relayed to him with the particular satisfaction of someone who'd spent a decade fighting a mechanism now finally, properly, working.

"You should be proud of that," she told him, watching rain sheet harmlessly off channels that would have been mud rivers three months earlier.

"I am," Leandro said, and found he genuinely meant it — not the quiet, private satisfaction of a personal project completed, but something larger, the accumulated weight of thousands of people who'd have suffered a hard winter made instead survivable through nothing more exotic than proper grading and patient, unglamorous labor.

Cassian, for his part, had quietly become something like a fixture in the camp district's nightly patrols, well-known enough now that people greeted him by name rather than as "the sword with the sarcastic mouth," a shift Leandro noted with private amusement and didn't comment on directly.

"We could stay," Cassian said one evening, watching the camp settle into its version of ordinary nightlife. "Ostwick's good to us. Good work here, if you wanted to keep doing this indefinitely."

Leandro considered it seriously, the way he considered most decisions. "We could," he agreed. "But Selvane's fall wasn't an isolated event. Whatever's coming next isn't going to wait for me to get comfortable in one city. I'd rather understand the shape of the whole board before I decide where I'm most useful."

Cassian studied him for a moment, then nodded slowly, recognizing the particular resolve that had been building in his friend since the night they'd first heard about Voss's stand. "Where to, then?"

"I don't know yet," Leandro admitted. "But I intend to find out."
