# Chapter 30 — The Deeper Push

Morning brought a unanimous, if cautious, decision — condition solid, supplies plentiful thanks to Leandro's preparation, no reason yet to turn back. They pushed onward from floor twenty-five with the same measured discipline that had carried them safely this far.

Floor twenty-six announced the dungeon's shifting character immediately — no longer isolated threats or clever environmental puzzles, but sheer, overwhelming numbers. A swarm of chittering, insectile creatures poured from fissures in the chamber walls the moment the party crossed its threshold, dozens at once, more than any single fighter could hope to individually track.

"Formation!" Doran called, and the party collapsed into a practiced defensive shape almost before Leandro fully processed the order — Bram anchoring the front, wide shield raised, absorbing the swarm's initial press with the sheer, immovable solidity of a man built specifically to be the wall everyone else fought behind.

"This is why you want a proper tank," Cassian shouted over the chittering din, blade shifted to its wider, sweeping form, cutting through whatever slipped past Bram's guard. "Somebody's got to be the thing they all try to hit first."

Percival was the one who truly turned the tide, though — a wide, sweeping arc of concentrated wind magic that scythed through the swarm's densest cluster in a single controlled burst, dozens of creatures falling at once where individual strikes would have taken minutes to achieve the same result.

"That's the difference a proper mage makes," Doran said afterward, watching Percival catch his breath, expression tight with the particular fatigue that came from sustained, high-output magic rather than physical exertion. "Swords and shields hold a line. But numbers like that need something that hits more than one enemy at a time, or you just grind yourselves down eventually, one kill at a time, until exhaustion does what the enemy couldn't."

Leandro absorbed the lesson with genuine attention, cataloguing it alongside everything else he was learning about this world's actual combat mechanics — his own water magic, he realized watching Percival work, had never been tested at that kind of scale, narrow and precise where true crowd control demanded breadth and force he simply didn't have the affinity to match.

"I could learn something from that," Leandro said, half to himself.

"You could," Percival agreed, overhearing. "Not the wind, probably — that's my affinity, not yours. But water can scale wide too, if you ever get the raw output for it. Floods aren't gentle things."

Leandro filed that away carefully, one more data point in the long, patient project of understanding his own limits and how they might, someday, expand.

Floors twenty-seven through thirty settled into a grinding, exhausting rhythm — swarm after swarm, the dungeon's design clearly favoring attrition over individual danger at this depth, testing endurance and resource management as much as raw combat skill. Bram's shield arm ached visibly by the fourth such fight, though he never once complained or asked for rest beyond what the party's schedule already allowed. Percival's magic output became the party's most carefully rationed resource, Doran calling for retreat-and-regroup tactics whenever a swarm proved larger than expected, unwilling to let his mage burn past a safe reserve no matter how the fight was going.

"Ration the mage before you ration anything else," Doran told Leandro plainly, during a quiet stretch between floors. "Swords and shields recover with rest. A mage pushed past their limit recovers slower, and a party without magical support at these depths isn't a party anymore. It's a countdown."

Leandro nodded slowly, already reorganizing his own mental framework around the lesson — utility was valuable, but raw magical output at scale was the party's actual bottleneck, the resource every strategic decision ultimately bent around protecting.

By floor thirty, exhausted but intact, the party found itself in another defensible rest chamber, considerably more battered than their stop at twenty-five had left them.

"Smithing cache is supposed to be right around here," Ilyana said, checking старые notes against the chamber's layout. "Assuming the old reports were accurate."

They found it the following morning — a sealed side chamber, its entrance marked with worn but still-legible symbols, containing a modest cache of ancient smithing tools and raw material the party had spent the better part of a year preparing to retrieve. Doran distributed what they found with careful, practiced fairness, though Leandro noted, with quiet private amusement, that nobody so much as glanced twice at his own ring throughout the entire process.

Floors thirty-one through thirty-five pushed the swarm pattern further still, chambers growing larger, enemy numbers thicker, each fight demanding tighter coordination than the last. It was floor thirty-three that gave Leandro his most meaningful combat contribution yet — a chamber flooding, quite literally, from some ancient cracked cistern feeding directly into the fight, water rising steadily around everyone's ankles as the swarm pressed in.

Where the rising water threatened to become a hazard for the rest of the party, footing growing treacherous, Leandro found he could simply direct it instead, channeling the flood into a controlled current that swept the swarm's leading edge back toward the chamber's far wall, buying the party the same kind of breathing room Percival's wind magic usually provided.

"Now that's more like it," Percival said, grinning despite his own exhaustion, watching Leandro's improvised flood do work far beyond anything his weak Novice affinity should reasonably manage on its own merit. "Environmental leverage. You're a slow study on raw output, Valdez, but you've got good instincts for using what's already in the room."

"I'll take the compliment," Leandro said, breathing hard, already cataloguing the experience for future reference — proof, however small, that his particular weakness didn't have to mean helplessness, so long as he kept finding rooms willing to lend him their own strength.

By floor thirty-five, the party settled into another hard-earned rest, wounded, tired, and steadily, cautiously proud of a depth that pushed well past anything Leandro and Cassian could have managed alone.

"Eleven floors past where we nearly died," Cassian said quietly, examining a fresh nick along his sword's edge with something like fondness. "It's been holding up well, this thing. Haven't even needed to see what else it can do yet."

Leandro glanced at the blade, still turning over in the back of his mind exactly what Voss had once said about it — shape adaptation, secondary runes, capabilities neither of them had fully explored. "Something tells me we haven't seen its limit yet."

"Something tells me you're right," Cassian said, sheathing it carefully. "Guess we'll find out when it actually matters."
