# Chapter 23 — Leaving Althea

The months following Ostwick's stabilization settled into a pattern Leandro recognized, uncomfortably, as its own kind of stagnation.

He'd done what he could — infrastructure work rippling outward from Ostwick to smaller towns along the western road, a growing, quiet reputation as "the outsider who fixes things," contracts that kept him and Cassian modestly funded without ever pushing either of them toward real danger. Demon Kin activity continued along Althea's fractured border, occasional raids probing weakened garrisons, occasional refugees arriving from towns Leandro had never gotten the chance to help before they burned. He'd traveled to two of them, once word reached him fast enough — found survivors already scattered, structures already lost, nothing left for his particular skills to meaningfully address beyond aiding whatever evacuation remained possible.

It wasn't enough, and he'd finally stopped pretending otherwise.

"You've been staring at that map for three days," Cassian said, watching Leandro trace the same worn routes across Althea's territory for what had to be the dozenth time that week.

"I've done everything I can here without exposing myself in ways I'm not ready for," Leandro said, frustration bleeding through his usual composed tone more than he intended. "I can't fight a raiding party. I can't hold a border the way Voss held a street. I can rebuild what's already been lost, and warn people to run before the next attack, and that's useful, Cassian, I know it's useful — but it's not enough, and continuing to do the same limited thing in the same limited radius isn't going to change that."

"So what does change it?"

Leandro was quiet for a long moment, turning the actual answer over before committing to it out loud. "I need more than what Althea's taught me. More combat capability, even a little — something beyond a weak Novice affinity and an ability that only works when I'm already dying. More understanding of what's actually possible in this world, magic and otherwise, that I haven't encountered yet because I've stayed inside one kingdom's borders since the day I arrived." He tapped the map, well beyond Althea's edges, toward territory neither of them had ever set foot in. "I want to see what else is out there. Properly. Not fleeing toward it because a city's fallen — going toward it, deliberately, because I think there's something to learn that I can't learn here."

Cassian studied the map for a long moment, then, unexpectedly, grinned. "You know, there's a town in Aldenreach I've been meaning to show you. Brindlemark. Guild's practically built the whole place around a nearby dungeon — been drawing adventurers from three kingdoms for as long as I can remember, chasing whatever's supposedly still unexplored down there. Loud, chaotic, full of the exact kind of people we just spent a season pulling out of collapsing buildings." He shrugged, easy and inviting. "Might be a good place to actually learn something. Or at least get properly laughed at by people who take dungeon delving more seriously than either of us has any right to."

Leandro allowed himself something close to genuine curiosity at that. "A dungeon."

"An actual, honest-to-nothing dungeon. Not a sealed cave that happens to have some old ruins in it — a proper, still-active site, refreshing itself over time, drawing adventurers who make an entire career out of pushing one floor deeper than the last group managed." Cassian's grin widened. "Brindlemark's a strange little town, Valdez. I think you'll either love it or find it completely insufferable. Possibly both, simultaneously."

"That's oddly specific."

"I've been there twice. Trust me."

They departed Ostwick a week later, Yara seeing them off with a grudging, genuine embrace and a muttered instruction not to get himself killed somewhere without a proper drainage system to fall back on. The road toward Aldenreach's border cut through territory neither of them had traveled, unfamiliar terrain that Leandro found himself cataloguing with fresh, restless attention after months of Althea's now-familiar roads.

Brindlemark announced itself long before they reached its gates — a haphazard sprawl of a town that had clearly grown far faster than any city planner had ever properly accounted for, buildings crammed together at odd angles, guild banners from at least four different kingdoms hanging in a market square built more for foot traffic than aesthetic order. The air buzzed with a particular restless energy Leandro hadn't felt since Selvane's guild hall in its calmer days — dozens of adventuring parties in various states of preparation, argument, celebration, or visible hangover, all orbiting the same singular gravity well: a dungeon entrance visible even from the town's edge, a dark, unassuming maw of stone set into a low hillside just beyond the outer walls.

"Welcome," Cassian said, spreading his arms with theatrical flourish, "to Brindlemark. Home to more terrible life decisions per capita than anywhere else I've ever had the pleasure of getting thrown out of."

A loud crash sounded from somewhere nearby, followed immediately by raucous laughter and someone shouting something about a "definitely-not-cursed" sword being sold at a suspiciously reasonable price. Leandro watched a party of adventurers — one of them apparently on fire, though nobody nearby seemed remotely concerned about it — stumble past in the general direction of the guild hall, still mid-argument about whose fault the fire actually was.

"You weren't exaggerating," Leandro said.

"I never do," Cassian said, grinning. "Come on. Let's find an inn before someone tries to sell you a 'guaranteed' treasure map. Happens to every newcomer within the first hour. It's practically a local tradition."

Leandro followed him into the chaos, feeling, for the first time in months, something loosen slightly in his chest — not resolution, not answers, but the genuine, cautious optimism of a man walking toward a problem he hadn't yet fully mapped, in a place that felt, against all reasonable expectation, like exactly the kind of chaotic, unglamorous testing ground he needed.
