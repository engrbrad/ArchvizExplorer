# Chapter 66 — The Long Walk Back

He couldn't move quickly, not with cracked ribs and a sprained leg protesting every step, and the mountain terrain surrounding the fortress offered no easy path back toward friendly territory. He spent the first hour simply orienting himself, using the fading stars and his own memory of the flight path to estimate a rough direction, before beginning the long, painful process of walking away from the wreckage he'd caused.

It took three days.

He rationed what little he'd carried on his person — water drawn from mountain streams when he found them, earth-shaped shelter scraped together each night with magic that cost him dearly given his injuries, phase never triggering despite several near-missteps on treacherous terrain, apparently unwilling to activate for anything less than genuine mortal danger. He passed within sight of at least two Demon Kin patrols during the walk, forced to hide, exhausted and in pain, until they moved on, unaware of exactly how close they'd come to the very person responsible for whatever chaos had clearly gripped their forces in the days since.

Word had already begun spreading, even in those first three days — he caught fragments of it from a distant patrol's agitated conversation, carried on mountain wind he was careful enough to make sure carried away from him rather than toward. Something about the stronghold. Something about their sovereign. Confusion, and beneath the confusion, something that sounded, even from a hostile source's mouth, like genuine fear.

He didn't fully understand what he'd done until he finally, exhausted and limping, reached a human border outpost on the fourth day, collapsing more than walking through its gates, and found the garrison already buzzing with news that had apparently reached them through channels faster than his own painful, cross-country trek.

"The Demon King is dead," a soldier told him, disbelief and cautious hope warring in her voice, before she'd even properly processed who this ragged, injured stranger stumbling through her gate actually was. "Nobody knows how. Some kind of massive explosion, tore the heart right out of their stronghold. Whole continent's talking about it already."

Leandro said nothing, simply nodding as though the news were new to him, and let the soldiers usher him toward medical care with the practiced efficiency of people who'd grown accustomed to processing the war's endless casualties.

He didn't tell them the truth. Not yet. Not until he'd had time to properly understand, himself, exactly what he'd just changed — and exactly what might come next, for a war whose entire shape had just been upended by a single, unexplained catastrophe nobody but Leandro and a small, trusted circle back at Ravencourt's workshop would ever fully understand the origin of.
