# Chapter 80 — Morning After

Leandro woke to the worst headache he could remember that didn't come from phasing — which, given everything his body had survived over the past six weeks, was saying something considerable.

He became aware of his surroundings in stages. First, the ceiling, unfamiliar for a disoriented moment before he placed it as the workshop's upper floor. Second, the distinct, unwelcome smell of last night's drink working its way back up through his own stomach in slow, threatening waves. Third, and most alarming, the pounding, all-too-familiar pressure behind his eyes that had absolutely nothing to do with alcohol.

That's a phase headache, he realized, with the particular dawning horror of a man piecing together a crime scene he'd apparently starred in. Why do I have a phase headache.

He had no memory of triggering it. None. The last clear recollection he could summon was Cassian's increasingly slurred toast to "unclaimed superpower potential," somewhere around the point the drink had thoroughly demolished his own usual careful restraint, and then — nothing. A gap. A void where several hours should have been.

He sat up too fast, immediately regretted it, and became aware of a second sound competing for his attention: retching, violent and prolonged, coming from somewhere down the hall.

He found Cassian in considerably worse condition, hunched over a basin that had clearly already failed to contain the full scope of the crisis, judging by the state of the floor around him.

"Please," Cassian managed, between heaves, "never let me drink Borgrim's 'saved for special occasions' bottle again. I have seen things. I have been things."

"What happened last night?" Leandro asked carefully, still probing at his own throbbing skull. "After the toast. I don't remember anything past that."

Cassian paused his ongoing crisis just long enough to squint up at him with bloodshot, deeply unhelpful eyes. "You don't remember?"

"No."

"...You fell off the workshop's back stairs," Cassian said slowly, memory apparently reassembling itself through considerable effort. "Face-first. Head-first, actually, I think, toward the stone landing at the bottom. I remember screaming your name. I remember thinking, very clearly, 'well, that's it, we survived the demon king and he's going to die falling down stairs drunk.'"

Leandro's stomach dropped, and not from the hangover. "And then?"

"And then you just—" Cassian made a vague, encompassing gesture, "—weren't there for a second. Flickered, or whatever it does. Landed at the bottom completely fine. I was so relieved I think I actually cried a little, and then I threw up on Borgrim's boots, and I don't remember much after that either."

Leandro closed his eyes, absorbing the full, mortifying shape of the story. "I triggered an involuntary, near-death survival ability," he said slowly, "by getting drunk enough to fall down a flight of stairs."

"In your defense," Cassian offered weakly, "it was a genuinely dangerous flight of stairs. Very steep. Poor lighting."

"That is not a defense, Cassian."

"It's the best I've got right now. I'm very fragile currently."

The rest of the household assembled slowly and miserably over the following hour, each arrival contributing another piece of the previous night's fog. Borgrim confirmed, with visible embarrassment, that yes, his boots had indeed suffered collateral damage, and that he'd been the one to half-carry both Leandro and Cassian to bed once the excitement had finally, mercifully concluded. Seraphina, considerably less hungover than the rest thanks to some minor healing magic applied to herself before bed, admitted with obvious guilt that she'd been the one distracted by Mirelle's conversation and hadn't noticed Leandro wander toward the stairs at all.

Mirelle, for her part, looked stricken the moment the full story assembled itself. "I was standing right there," she said quietly. "I should have noticed. After everything he survived getting back here—"

"Nobody could have predicted a man phasing through mortal danger via drunken stair mishap," Cassian said, still pale but recovering enough for dry humor. "That's not a failure of vigilance. That's just an absurd way for the universe to test us."

"It's not funny," Mirelle said, though something in her tone suggested she was fighting not to find it exactly that, guilt and reluctant humor visibly warring behind her expression.

"It's a little funny," Borgrim admitted gruffly. "In hindsight. Once we've confirmed nobody's actually dead."

Leandro, sitting through the whole mortifying post-mortem with his head in his hands, finally looked up. "Can we please move on from the story of how I nearly died celebrating my own survival, and focus on something considerably more productive?"

They gathered properly an hour later, once food, water, and a round of Seraphina's minor headache remedies had restored enough collective functionality for serious conversation, and the mood shifted, soberly, into the planning session the previous night's celebration had delayed.

"We have one dragon stone remaining," Leandro said, laying out the practical shape of what came next. "The Demon King is dead, their Court's thrown into disarray, their advance halted. That's more than I hoped for when we started this. But nine generals remain, an entire army remains, and whatever internal instability we've caused won't last indefinitely."

"You also learned something that changes how I think about all of this," Seraphina said carefully. "The famine. The reason behind the invasion in the first place."

"I have," Leandro admitted. "I don't have a clean answer for what that means yet. I don't think ending this war purely through more weapons, more deaths on their side, actually resolves anything long-term if the underlying cause — starvation, desperation — remains untouched. But I also can't unmake what's already been done, or pretend Selvane and everyone this war has cost don't deserve some measure of justice."

"That's a heavier question than any of us can answer this morning," Mirelle said quietly. "Hungover and exhausted isn't when good policy gets decided."

"Fair," Leandro said, managing a tired, wry smile. "Then let's start smaller. Practical steps. What does the team actually need, going forward?"

Borgrim was first to answer, gruff and practical as always. "Better containment work, if we're building toward the last dragon stone properly. And I want a real conversation, sober, about whether that stone gets used at all, or held in reserve, given everything you just told us."

"I want to understand the famine better," Seraphina added. "If it's real, if it's driving the whole war, that's information that could reshape strategy across every human kingdom, assuming anyone would actually listen to it."

"And I," Mirelle said, some of her usual precise composure returning now that the conversation had moved to solid, familiar ground, "want considerably more data before anyone makes another dramatic, unilateral decision to fly a bomb into enemy territory alone. That plan worked. It also very nearly didn't, and I would prefer this team's next major decision not rest entirely on one man's individual survival odds."

Leandro absorbed that, the fair, pointed weight of it landing properly despite his lingering headache. "That's reasonable," he admitted. "I won't promise I'll never act alone again. But I'll promise to actually discuss it with all of you first, rather than simply deciding and executing."

"That's all we're asking," Cassian said, still pale but steady. "Preferably decisions that don't also involve staircases."

"Agreed," Leandro said, and the tension of the morning finally, properly broke into something like renewed, careful resolve — a team, bruised and hungover and still processing everything the past six weeks had cost and revealed, beginning, together, to plan what came next.
