# Chapter 42 — Farewells

The parting from Doran's party happened over a final shared meal at the guild hall, less ceremony than genuine, comfortable closure between people who'd trusted each other with their lives across fifty-one increasingly dangerous floors.

"You could stay," Percival said, only half-joking. "We could use a permanent logistics specialist who happens to also throw water at problems until they stop being problems."

"I'm flattered," Leandro said. "But I think there's a different kind of usefulness I need to go find."

Doran nodded slowly, unsurprised. "The war."

"The war," Leandro confirmed. "I've spent months proving I can matter without a sword. I want to see if that's still true somewhere the stakes are worse than a dungeon floor."

Ilyana studied the two of them for a moment, something thoughtful behind her expression. "For what it's worth — we've been talking, since the broadsheets came in. Fifty-one floors, and we still walked away feeling like we'd barely scratched what's actually out there. Reading about generals falling, towns burning, people half our strength holding lines we're not sure we could hold ourselves." She glanced at Doran, some unspoken agreement passing between them. "We're thinking of heading toward the front too. Not recklessly. But sitting in a dungeon getting stronger for its own sake feels different, now, than it did a season ago."

"Fame's a strange thing when it doesn't actually change anything," Doran added quietly. "We're locally well-regarded. Guild's proud of us. None of that mattered to a single town that burned while we were fifty floors underground chasing our own advancement. That sits wrong, the longer I think about it."

Bram, characteristically brief, simply nodded once. "There's always someone stronger. Doesn't mean we shouldn't try to matter anyway."

Cassian raised his cup toward all four of them. "To finding out what we're actually worth, then. Wherever that ends up taking any of us."

They drank to that, and parted the following morning with the easy, mutual respect of people who suspected, correctly, that their paths might cross again somewhere further down a much larger board.
