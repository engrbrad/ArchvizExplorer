The commission board in Selvane's guild hall had a dozen postings that morning, most of them the usual — pest control, escort work, delivery runs — but one caught Cassian's eye immediately, tapping it twice with a grin Leandro had learned to be suspicious of.

"Ancient cave," Cassian read aloud. "Recently uncovered near a village called Millbrook, three days east. Guild wants a survey team — not full excavation, just enough to flag anything dangerous or valuable before the scholars descend on it. Pays decent, and—" he glanced sideways at Leandro, "—historically, ruins like this sometimes turn up old relics. Magic items. The kind of thing that doesn't exist anymore because nobody remembers how to make it."

Leandro's interest was more clinical than treasure-driven — an untouched, sealed structure was data, plain and simple, a chance to see something built by people who might have understood this world's mechanics in ways no living scholar currently did. "I'm in," he said, and Cassian looked entirely too pleased with himself for a man who hadn't actually needed to convince him.

They took the contract together, along with two guild-assigned surveyors more interested in cataloguing than fighting, and set out east the following morning.

Millbrook, when they finally reached it after three days of increasingly rough road, was not what Leandro expected from a village important enough to warrant a guild commission.

It should have been thriving, at least modestly — proximity to a fresh archaeological discovery usually meant increased traffic, coin, attention. Instead, the first thing Leandro registered, with the same automatic assessment he'd apply to any facility walkthrough, was how much was visibly wrong.

Half the roofs needed re-thatching, patched with mismatched material that wouldn't survive a hard rain. The central well — he clocked it immediately, old habits from lessons on Ostwick's water infrastructure already forming — had a cracked stone rim and a bucket mechanism that groaned audibly even at rest. Fields on the village's edge were only half-tended, weeds choking rows that should have been cleared weeks ago. And the people themselves moved with the particular tired wariness of a community that had been dealing with more than one problem for longer than they had the resources to handle.

"This doesn't look like a village excited about ancient treasure next door," Cassian muttered, echoing Leandro's own read on the place.

The village elder who met them — a stooped, gray-haired woman named Petra, who introduced herself with the flat exhaustion of someone who'd explained the situation too many times already — confirmed it within minutes.

"The cave's real enough," Petra said, leading them toward what passed for a communal hall. "Found it three weeks back when the Halvern boy's goat wandered into a hole and didn't come back out easy.
