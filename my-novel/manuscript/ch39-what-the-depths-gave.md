# Chapter 39 — What the Depths Gave

The golem chamber, once fully cleared, revealed its own cache tucked behind a collapsed section of wall — the dungeon's apparent pattern of rewarding each major threshold with something proportionate to the difficulty of reaching it. Combined with smaller finds accumulated across floors forty-six through forty-nine — refined monster crystals from the broodmother, salvageable materials from the giants' den, a scattering of enchanted feathers from the harpies that Percival immediately recognized as valuable spellcasting components, and a handful of unusually structured slime cores that Ferrin back in Brindlemark would likely pay well to study — the party found itself, for the first time since beginning this expedition, sitting on genuine, substantial wealth.

They divided most of it with practiced, easy fairness, the same unbothered system Leandro had noticed from his very first days tagging along at floor fifteen. But it was the golem chamber's central item that drew the party into a longer, more serious conversation than any previous discovery had prompted.

It rested in a sealed alcove behind the last golem's collapse — a small, rough-hewn stone carving, warm to the touch despite the surrounding chamber's cold, radiating a faint, steady pulse that Leandro recognized, the moment he laid a hand on it, as unmistakably kin to his own earth affinity.

"Earth spirit fragment," Doran said, examining it with careful reverence. "I've heard of these. Rare beyond rare — said to accelerate and expand whatever earth affinity the bearer already carries, sometimes dramatically, depending on how compatible the spirit's nature is with the wielder's own."

"That's yours," Ilyana said immediately, glancing toward Leandro without a moment's hesitation.

"You don't have to decide that so fast," Leandro said, genuinely caught off guard. "There's five of you with legitimate claim to anything found down here."

"None of the rest of us carry earth affinity at all," Percival pointed out. "It'd sit in someone's storage as a curiosity. In your hands, it's an actual amplifier for a magic you've clearly been building from nothing this whole expedition." He shrugged, easy and unbothered. "We don't need it. You do. That's not charity, Valdez. That's just sense."

Bram nodded once, characteristically brief agreement. Doran and Cassian exchanged a glance that settled the matter without further debate needed.

Leandro accepted it carefully, the same deliberate caution he'd once shown the ring, and felt, the moment his palm closed fully around the fragment, something shift and settle deep beneath his sternum — not violent, not overwhelming, but unmistakably real, a quiet, steady expansion of the same faint awareness he'd carried since first compacting loose earth beneath a canal wall months ago.

"That's... considerable," he said slowly, testing the sensation with cautious, deliberate focus. A small trial shaping, barely more than instinct, produced a result noticeably beyond anything he'd managed before — cleaner, faster, costing visibly less of the bone-deep exhaustion earth magic had always demanded of him.

"Welcome to slightly less pathetic Novice status," Cassian said, grinning despite his own exhaustion.

"I'll take it," Leandro said, genuinely moved by the gesture, turning the fragment over once more before carefully storing it. "Thank you. All of you."

"Don't get sentimental on us," Doran said, though his tone carried real warmth beneath the deflection. "You've more than earned it twice over, given everything you've done to keep this team alive down here."
