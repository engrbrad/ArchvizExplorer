# Chapter 33 — The Nest

Floor forty-six opened into thick, suffocating webbing that coated the passage walls like a second layer of stone, faint chittering echoing from unseen depths the moment the party crossed the threshold.

"Slow and controlled," Doran said, drawing his sword — a slender blade that caught the dim light with a faint, warm luminescence, light magic humming quietly along its edge. "Whatever's nesting here has had this whole floor to itself. We're the intrusion."

They found the first spiders within minutes — smaller than Leandro expected, barely larger than a dog, but fast, and their bite proved its danger almost immediately when one lunged past Bram's guard and grazed Ilyana's forearm before Cassian's blade removed it entirely.

"Antidote," Leandro said, already moving, Ferrin's careful formula administered within seconds of the bite. Ilyana's breathing steadied, though not before a visible, frightening moment where her sword arm went briefly numb.

"That's fast-acting venom," Percival said grimly. "Faster than the reports suggested."

They pressed on, and the floor punished every subsequent minute of progress. A second bite took Bram along his unarmored neckline, the venom's effect compounding faster than Leandro's antidote could fully counter, leaving the big man's movements sluggish for the better part of an hour despite treatment. A third bite caught Doran himself, shallow but enough to blur his vision briefly mid-fight, forcing Percival to burn a precious reserve of magic clearing a swarm that should have been Doran's alone to handle.

By the time they reached the nest's central chamber, Leandro's antidote stock — carefully rationed, meticulously planned — had thinned to barely three doses remaining, considerably faster than his projections had accounted for.

"We're running low," Leandro said quietly to Doran, checking the remaining supply with growing unease. "I planned for this floor's expected difficulty. This is worse than expected."

"Then we finish this quickly," Doran said. "No more attrition than we can afford."

The central chamber, when they finally reached it, revealed exactly what had been guarding this depth from every prior expedition.

The broodmother was larger than anything the party had faced yet — a monstrous, bloated shape easily the size of a small house, eight legs each ending in barbed, venom-slick points, surrounded by a dense scattering of egg sacs pulsing faintly in the dim light. It rose at their entrance with a shriek that sent the smaller spiders scattering into defensive positions around it, and the fight that followed was unlike anything they'd survived to that point.

Bram anchored the front as always, but the broodmother's reach and speed outmatched anything his shield had faced before, forcing him into a desperate, reactive defense rather than his usual immovable stance. Ilyana circled for openings that barely existed, the creature's carapace deflecting strikes that would have felled lesser monsters outright. Percival's wind magic tore through the smaller spiders in efficient bursts, but the broodmother itself shrugged off spell after spell with barely a flinch, its size and resilience simply outscaling what the mage could bring to bear alone.

It was Doran who found the first real opening — light magic flaring along his blade's edge, a precise, blinding strike aimed at one of the creature's many eyes that forced a genuine, pained shriek from the beast for the first time in the fight.

"There!" Doran shouted, pressing the advantage, driving in close with a speed and precision that made his earlier, more measured fighting look almost restrained by comparison. "It's vulnerable there—"

The broodmother's leg came down before he could fully retreat from the follow-up strike, barbed and impossibly fast, catching his blade dead-center in a downward strike that drove it deep into the creature's own carapace — and held it there, wedged beyond any hope of a clean retrieval, as the leg wrenched sideways and tore the weapon from Doran's grip entirely.

"Doran!" Ilyana's voice cracked with real fear, watching him stumble back, weaponless, directly into the path of a second leg already swinging toward him.

He didn't have time to draw a backup blade. He barely had time to raise his bare hands, light magic flaring instinctively around his forearms in a desperate, half-formed barrier that absorbed the worst of the blow but still sent him crashing hard into the chamber wall, breath driven from his lungs entirely.

"Cover him!" Bram roared, throwing himself bodily into the broodmother's path, shield raised against an attack meant for someone already down.

Leandro moved without fully deciding to, phase flickering unbidden as a stray leg swept toward him mid-sprint toward Doran's fallen position — the familiar spike of pain dropping him to one knee even as he reached the guildmaster's side, hands already working antidote into a fresh bite wound he hadn't even registered Doran taking.

"Stay down," Leandro said, voice tight with his own pain, forcing the words out through the headache's crushing pressure. "Let them hold it."

"My sword—" Doran gasped, staring past Leandro toward the broodmother, his blade still lodged uselessly in its carapace, entirely out of reach.

"Forget the sword," Leandro said. "You're weaponless and poisoned. That's the priority."

It was Cassian who saw the opening the rest of the party couldn't.

Percival, cornered by a fresh wave of smaller spiders while trying to line up another wind-based strike against the broodmother, found his usual spellwork simply insufficient against a target this large and this resilient — raw magic alone, without a proper conduit to focus and channel it, scattering too much of its force to matter against something this size.

"Percival!" Cassian's voice cut across the chamber, already moving, sword drawn and thrown in a single fluid motion that closed the distance faster than any run could have managed. "Catch!"

The blade landed hilt-first in Percival's outstretched hand, and the moment his fingers closed around it, something in the weapon responded — not to Cassian's familiar, practiced intent this time, but to an entirely different kind of will, wind-affinity magic flooding into a conduit that had never carried elemental force before and finding, to everyone's shock, that it welcomed the addition eagerly.

The blade grew, shifting and lengthening under Percival's grip into something far beyond any of its previous forms — a great, elegant length of gleaming metal wreathed now in visible, howling wind, Percival's own magic amplified and focused through a weapon clearly built, whatever ancient hands had forged it, to make its wielder simply more than they'd been a moment before.

"—by the depths—" Percival breathed, staring at the transformed blade in his hands with pure astonishment, power thrumming through it in a way his own small storage case or modest spellcasting had never remotely suggested was possible.

"Use it!" Cassian shouted, already moving to cover Doran and Leandro both, sword now gone from his own grip but his earth affinity flaring instead, a rough, hastily shaped wall of compacted stone rising to block the broodmother's next strike, weak and imperfect but enough, for one crucial moment, to buy time.

Percival didn't hesitate further. He surged forward, the great wind-wreathed blade catching the low light as he drove it in a single, sweeping arc directly into the wound Doran's own strike had already opened along the broodmother's eye — wind magic no longer scattered and diffuse but focused, amplified, and lethal, tearing through carapace and flesh alike with a force that made the creature's earlier shriek sound like a whisper by comparison.

The broodmother's death throes shook the entire chamber, legs thrashing wildly before finally, mercifully, going still, the great bloated shape collapsing in on itself with a final, shuddering exhale that stirred the surrounding webbing like a dying wind.

Silence, heavy and disbelieving, settled over the chamber in its wake.

Percival stood motionless for a long moment, still staring at the blade in his hands, breathing hard, before he seemed to remember himself and carefully, almost reverently, extended it back toward Cassian.

"That," Percival said, voice unsteady, "was not supposed to be possible with a weapon like this."

"Apparently it disagrees with you," Cassian said, taking the blade back with visible exhaustion of his own, watching it shrink and settle back into its familiar sword shape the moment his own hand closed around the hilt. "Didn't know it could do that, for the record. Wasn't a plan. Just... the only thing I had left to throw at the problem."

"It amplified me," Percival said, still processing. "Not just gave me a bigger weapon. I could feel my own magic responding differently the second I held it — stronger, more focused, like the blade itself wanted to help rather than just serve as a conduit."

Leandro, still kneeling beside Doran and administering the last of their antidote reserves, glanced up at the exchange with quiet, fascinated attention despite his own lingering headache. "That's worth understanding properly, later. For now—" he turned back to Doran, checking his vitals with careful focus, "—how are you feeling?"

"Like I got thrown into a wall by something the size of a house," Doran said, wincing as he tested his own ribs gingerly. "Which, to be fair, is exactly what happened." He looked toward his own sword, still lodged uselessly in the broodmother's now-still carapace, and let out a short, disbelieving laugh. "I lost my blade in the first real fight this floor's given us, and got saved by someone else's weapon changing hands twice in the same breath. Not exactly how I imagined surviving this floor."

"You held the fight together before that happened," Ilyana said, crouching beside him with visible relief plain on her face. "Opened the first real wound in that thing. Bought Percival the time he needed to close the rest. That's not a small thing, Doran, whatever you lost getting there."

Bram, still catching his breath from holding the line, managed a rare, small smile. "Whole team held. Nobody died. That's the only scorecard that matters."

Leandro finished his check on Doran, satisfied the venom's spread had been stopped in time, and allowed himself, finally, a slow, exhausted breath of his own. "Floor forty-six," he said quietly, looking around at a party bruised, poisoned, and thoroughly spent, but alive. "Cleared."

Nobody celebrated loudly. There wasn't much energy left for it. But the quiet, shared relief that settled over the chamber said everything the exhaustion didn't allow anyone to voice properly.
