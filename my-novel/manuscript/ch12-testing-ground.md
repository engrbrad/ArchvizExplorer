# Chapter 12 — Testing Ground

They gave themselves a week in Selvane before doing anything more ambitious than sleeping properly and eating food that hadn't been rationed for a cave expedition. Leandro spent most of it quietly cataloguing — not villages this time, but the ring itself, treating it with the same patient, methodical curiosity he'd once reserved for an unfamiliar control panel.

He started small, in the privacy of the room he and Cassian shared above the guild's attached inn. A change of clothes. A few days' rations. His notebook, the one full of gear ratios and half-finished sketches, tucked away and retrieved again just to confirm the pages hadn't shifted, hadn't aged, hadn't so much as gathered a speck of dust in whatever space the ring kept them.

"Time doesn't move in there," he said, more to himself than to Cassian, turning a piece of bread over in his hands after pulling it from storage a full day after putting it in. Still warm. Still soft. "Or it moves so slowly it doesn't matter at the scale I can test."

"That's not just convenient," Cassian said, watching from his bed with the sword laid across his lap, still turning it over like he half-expected it to do something new every time he looked at it. "That's absurd, Valdez. Do you understand how much coin a merchant would pay for a way to keep goods fresh indefinitely?"

"I understand," Leandro said. "I also understand why I'm not telling anyone about it."

He spent the rest of that first day testing capacity more rigorously — not with idle curiosity now, but with the same systematic thoroughness he'd apply to stress-testing any new piece of equipment before trusting it under load. Water first, since it was easiest to measure and closest to his own affinity: a full barrel, stored and retrieved without so much as a drop lost or a change in temperature. Then stone, then metal scrap borrowed from a smith's discard pile, testing weight rather than volume, trying to find where the ring's limits actually sat rather than assuming based on impression alone.

By the end of the week, he had a working, if incomplete, picture: substantial capacity, no apparent weight limit he'd yet found a way to test to failure, and — the detail that mattered most for what he was already quietly planning — perfect preservation for anything organic. Not just convenient. Foundational, for a man who'd spent the last month learning that half of rural Althea's problems traced back to some version of "we can't move or keep enough of what we need, fast enough, before it spoils or runs out."

He didn't say any of that out loud to Cassian yet. He just kept a private list, the way he always did, and let the implications settle before acting on them.

Cassian's testing was louder, and considerably more entertaining to watch.

They found a quiet, disused training yard on Selvane's outskirts, the kind of place adventurers used to spar without an audience, and Cassian spent the better part of two afternoons simply holding the sword, trying every mental instruction he could think of to coax the promised shape-change out of it.

"Spear," he said firmly, glaring at the blade like it had personally wronged him. Nothing happened. "Longer. Just— longer, then."

Nothing.

"Maybe it's not intent-based like the ring," Leandro offered, watching from a bench with genuine scientific interest now that his own testing had reached a temporary plateau. "Try physical input instead. Force, maybe. Or a specific motion."

Cassian tried swinging it in increasingly exaggerated arcs, muttering half-serious commands under his breath, until — on a wide, frustrated overhead swing that put considerably more force behind it than any of his careful attempts — the blade shifted mid-motion, lengthening with a soft metallic groan into something closer to a naginata, the added reach nearly costing him his balance entirely.

"—whoa—" Cassian staggered, overcorrecting, and Leandro was on his feet in an instant more out of instinct than any real fear the man would hurt himself.

"Stop moving," Leandro said. "Just hold it still and tell me what you feel."

Cassian steadied, breathing hard, staring down at the transformed blade with wide-eyed fascination. "It's— heavier at the tip now. Balanced different. Feels right, though, the same way it did when I first picked it up. Like it just... knew what I needed halfway through that swing."

"Reactive, then," Leandro said, already turning the mechanism over in his head. "Not commanded outright — responsive to combat need, maybe reading momentum or intent-in-motion rather than intent-at-rest. That would explain why standing still and talking to it did nothing." He studied the naginata form with open curiosity. "Can you shift it back?"

Cassian tried, swinging it through a tighter, more controlled arc this time, and the blade obliged, shortening back into its original sword shape with the same soft groan.

"That," Cassian said, grinning now despite the sweat on his brow, "might be the single coolest thing that has ever happened to me. And I include the chimera in that assessment."

They spent the rest of the week like that — modest, private testing, refining a working understanding of what each item could and couldn't do, careful never to push too hard in public view. Leandro kept his storage box use strictly out of sight, a habit he suspected would need to last a very long time. Cassian, for his part, discovered the sword could shift into at least three distinct forms depending on the type of motion behind the trigger — a spear-length reach, a shorter, faster dueling blade, and, memorably, something closer to a full battle-axe that nearly took out a fence post before he learned to control the transition properly.

By the end of the week, tired, sore, and considerably better informed about exactly what the cave had cost and given them in equal measure, they took one final, smaller contract together — a modest E-rank job clearing minor pests from a farmstead just outside Selvane's walls — mostly to confirm, quietly and without fanfare, that both of them could still function as an ordinary, unremarkable adventuring pair when the situation called for it.

It went, blessedly, without incident. No traps. No ancient wards. Just a straightforward job, done competently, for a farmer who thanked them with a warm meal and no questions about the strange sword or the odd ring he hadn't noticed at all.

"Normal," Cassian said afterward, stretching out under the evening sky with the particular satisfaction of a man appreciating boredom for the first time in weeks. "I'd almost forgotten what that felt like."

"Enjoy it," Leandro said, watching the sunset with his own quiet, unhurried contentment. "I have a feeling it won't last."

He wasn't wrong, though neither of them knew it yet.
