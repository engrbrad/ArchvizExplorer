# Chapter 14 — Ostwick

Ostwick announced itself before the city walls did — the smell of brine and wet stone, the distant clank of lock mechanisms, and a skyline dominated not by spires or palaces but by grain silos, crane towers, and the crossed silhouettes of masts crowding a harbor busier than anything Leandro had seen since arriving in this world.

"Merchant city," Cassian said, watching Leandro's eyes track the harbor's mechanical bones with obvious hunger. "Consortium runs it more than any lord does. Adventurer's guild's got a satellite office here, but it's small potatoes compared to the trade houses. You're going to love it and also probably get yourself in trouble fixing something nobody asked you to fix within the first week. I give it three days, personally."

"I'll take that bet," Leandro said, already half-distracted by the lock system visible from the road — a staged series of gated chambers raising and lowering river traffic against the tidal delta, clever in principle, and, from what he could already see even at a distance, badly maintained in practice.

He lost the bet in two.

The lock keeper, a weathered woman named Yara who'd run the eastern gate mechanism for eleven years, was initially unimpressed by an outsider adventurer expressing polite curiosity about her infrastructure — until Leandro asked a question specific enough to prove he wasn't just making conversation.

"Your upper gate's lagging the lower one by almost a full count on every full cycle," he said, watching the mechanism labor through a lift. "That's not intentional, is it?"

Yara's expression shifted from polite dismissal to something sharper. "Been that way since before I took the post. Everyone says it's just how the mechanism's always run."

"It's a timing mismatch in the counterweight release," Leandro said. "Probably wasn't always that bad — wear accumulates asymmetrically if the load's uneven, and it compounds over years. I'd want to look at the gearing directly before I say more, but that's my guess."

It took him the better part of two weeks, working alongside Yara and a pair of consortium-employed mechanics who treated him with the particular wary respect reserved for outsiders who turned out to actually know what they were talking about, but the fix — once diagnosed properly — was almost embarrassingly simple: a re-cut gear tooth profile, a rebalanced counterweight, and a full cycle that ran, for the first time in over a decade, in genuine sync.

Word of it reached the consortium offices faster than Leandro expected. He turned down, politely but firmly, an offer of formal employment almost identical in shape to the one he'd declined in Selvane, for almost identical reasons.

"You have a type," Cassian said again, watching him decline yet another comfortable position with the same easy conviction as always.

"I have a plan," Leandro corrected. "It's just slower than most people's."

It was in Ostwick's crowded harbor tavern, a few weeks into their stay, that they first heard her name properly — not as a rumor exactly, but as the kind of story sailors and merchants told each other over drinks, half-embellished, wholly admiring.

"—burned through a whole nest of them in one pass, they're saying, out past Ravencourt's eastern hold. Fire mage, near Master rank, they call her the Ember Blade in some circles—"

"Bit dramatic a name," Cassian murmured to Leandro, though his eyes had gone sharp with interest.

"—single-handedly held a collapsing bridge crossing against a beast pack while her party evacuated a whole caravan. Ravencourt's academy's been trying to recruit her permanent-like for years, but word is she doesn't sit still long enough for anyone to pin her down."

Leandro filed the name away without much ceremony at the time — one interesting story among the dozen he heard in any given week of travel — though Cassian brought her up again, unprompted, a few days later.

"Ember Blade," Cassian said, turning the name over. "If half those stories are true, that's someone worth knowing eventually. Not now. But eventually."

"Noted," Leandro said, and didn't think much more of it, too absorbed in Ostwick's lock system and its growing list of smaller, adjacent problems to give a stranger's reputation more attention than it was due.
