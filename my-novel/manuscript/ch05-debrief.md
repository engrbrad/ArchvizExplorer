# Chapter 5 — Debrief

The village was smaller up close than Leandro's headache-addled brain had first registered — a cluster of thatched roofs and worried faces that lit with something close to relief when Cassian's cart rolled in with a dead three-horn bear strapped to the back and one very alive, very pale outsider propped against a sack of grain.

The missing child, it turned out, hadn't needed rescuing in the traditional sense — she'd fallen into an old grain cellar two farms over, twisted an ankle, and been too scared to shout loud enough to be heard, a fact the search party discovered while Leandro was still unconscious in the cart. Cassian delivered the news with the specific relief of a man who hadn't wanted to add "failed to find the kid" to an already disastrous contract report.

"So we win," Cassian said later, once Leandro could sit upright without the world tilting. "Bear's dead, kid's fine, village is happy. Technically a success."

"Technically," Leandro echoed, unconvinced.

He didn't say the rest out loud yet — that from his own accounting, the mission had gone about as poorly as it possibly could have for him personally. He hadn't fought. He hadn't contributed. He'd been carried through most of it unconscious, alive by an accident of whatever his body had done without his permission, while the actual danger got handled entirely by someone else. In the tally he kept in his head — the same honest, unflinching self-review he'd apply to any piece of equipment that underperformed — the result was clear: water magic, negligible combat value. Phase ability, uncontrollable, unreliable, debilitating on use. Net contribution to survival: someone else's sword.

It didn't demoralize him. It clarified something.

Back at the guild hall in Selvane three days later, contract closed and payment split, Cassian brought it up again over drinks Leandro wasn't drinking.

"You went through a bear," Cassian said, low enough that the tavern noise covered it. "Multiple times. That's not water magic. That's not anything I've heard of, and I've heard of a lot of things."

"I know."

"You gonna tell me what it actually is?"

Leandro considered the question carefully, the way he considered any decision with downstream consequences he couldn't fully predict yet. He didn't distrust Cassian, exactly — if anything, the man had more reason to be owed an explanation than almost anyone else in this world, having watched it happen up close and then hauled the aftermath home in a cart. But there was a difference between owing someone honesty and broadcasting a vulnerability to a world that had already tried, once, to summon someone specifically because of what they might be capable of.

"Something triggers when I'm about to die," Leandro said finally. "It's not something I control. It doesn't help me fight. It just... happens, and then it costs me, badly, afterward. That's the whole picture, as far as I understand it."

Cassian turned his cup slowly on the table. "That's not nothing, Valdez. That's the kind of thing that gets a man very famous, very fast, in the wrong circles."

"Which is exactly why I want it to stay quiet." Leandro met his eyes, serious now in a way that cut through the easy rapport they'd built over the past weeks. "Think about it from the outside. A man who can't be killed. No kingdom, no noble house, no ambitious general is going to hear that and think 'let's leave him alone.' They're going to think leverage. A living shield. A test subject. A weapon, if they can figure out how to point it at something. I don't know the limits of this thing yet — I don't know if it'll hold up against something smarter than a bear, or someone specifically trying to exploit it. Until I do, the only safe move is nobody knowing it exists at all."

"Even the guild?"

"Especially the guild." Leandro's voice was flat, certain. "Rank records, contract logs, reputation — all of that eventually reaches people with more power and less patience than a village elder. I'd rather be known as forgettably weak than dangerously interesting. Weak, people leave alone. Interesting, people take."

Cassian studied him for a long moment, something calculating behind the usual easy charm — the same look, Leandro was beginning to realize, that meant Cassian was actually being serious rather than performing his usual chaos.

"You really have thought this through," Cassian said.

"I think about consequences for a living," Leandro said. "Used to, anyway."

Cassian huffed something like a laugh, but it didn't have much humor in it. "Alright. Your secret. I won't touch it." He raised his cup in a mock toast. "To Leandro Valdez — official record: weak, unremarkable water mage, occasionally gets stepped on by bears and somehow lives anyway. Nobody needs to know the how."

Leandro allowed himself something close to a real, tired smile. "That's the version I can live with."

"Funny thing to say," Cassian said, "for a guy who apparently doesn't have to worry about dying at all."

"I'd still rather not find the actual edges of that the hard way again," Leandro said, rubbing at his temple where the ghost of the headache still lingered. "Once was plenty."
