# Chapter 22 — The Second Seat

He arrived late, as he always did, and the chamber's temperature seemed to drop by unspoken agreement rather than any actual chill in the air — a dozen generals adjusting posture, conversations trailing off mid-sentence, the collective, involuntary recalibration of creatures who could feel, without needing to look, exactly what had just walked through the door.

Malgorath took his seat — the Second, carved larger and set slightly apart from the others, a detail nobody had ever needed to explain to a new arrival — and said nothing at all for the first quarter hour of the meeting, apparently more interested in turning a small, unremarkable stone over in his claws than in anything the Court discussed around him.

Vharek had long since stopped expecting otherwise. "Second Seat. Your forces remain in reserve, as requested?"

"Mm." Malgorath didn't look up from the stone. "Wherever you last put them, presumably."

Kaelthorn, seated close enough to catch the exchange, made the mistake — as he occasionally still did, despite years of evidence advising against it — of pressing further. "Don't you care how the campaign proceeds? Selvane has fallen. We stand closer to breaking these kingdoms than any generation before us has managed."

Malgorath's attention shifted, slow and unhurried, to the Twelfth Seat — and Kaelthorn's posture changed instantly, some deep instinctive part of him recognizing, even before conscious thought caught up, exactly how far beneath this particular gaze he actually sat.

"Should I?" Malgorath asked, without malice, without any real weight behind the question at all — which was, somehow, worse than if he'd sounded threatening. "Kingdoms rise. Kingdoms fall. I've watched it happen more times than this Court has existed in its current form, under names none of you would recognize now, for reasons that felt just as urgent to the generals arguing about them at the time." He turned the stone over once more. "It will happen again, with or without my particular attention to the schedule."

"Then why sit on this Court at all?" Kaelthorn's voice had lost most of its earlier edge, replaced by something closer to genuine, unguarded confusion.

"Because Vharek asks politely, and I find I have no strong objection to occupying a chair once a month," Malgorath said, entirely serious. "If that changes, I imagine you'll all notice rather quickly."

Nobody at the table needed clarification on what "notice" would actually entail. The silence that followed had a different texture than Kaelthorn's usual uncomfortable retreats — heavier, more total, the particular quiet of a room full of powerful creatures collectively deciding not to test a boundary none of them had ever seen the far edge of.

Vharek let the moment pass without comment, redirecting the Court's attention back toward the map, though he found his own thoughts lingering on the exchange longer than he'd have liked. Malgorath's apathy was, in its own way, more useful to the campaign than any general's active ambition — an entity of that magnitude, invested in the outcome, would have ended this war in a season, human resistance or no. His disinterest was the only reason the Court's careful, patient strategy mattered at all. Should that disinterest ever shift, for reasons of his own that nobody at this table would be permitted to question, the entire calculated grind of the campaign would become, retroactively, an elaborate irrelevance.

A blessing and a threat, wearing the same face, Vharek thought, watching the Second Seat return his attention to the stone in his claws, already disengaged from a conversation that, to him, plainly did not warrant further consideration.

Later — Court business, resumed

"The matter of the humans' individual strength bears addressing properly," Vharek said, once discussion moved past the uncomfortable silence Malgorath's presence always left behind. "Serai's intelligence, and the Ninth Seat's own fate, both confirm what I suspect several of you would prefer to dismiss: pockets of genuine capability exist within human ranks. Not en masse — their kingdoms remain, on the whole, exactly as fragile as we've assessed. But individuals. A guildmaster capable of felling one of the Twelve outright. Rumors, growing rather than fading, of a mage in the west whose strength Morvain himself rates as considerable. Others, likely, that we have not yet identified."

"Isolated anomalies," one of the more dismissive generals offered. "Every generation produces a handful of exceptional humans. It has never meaningfully altered the outcome of any previous campaign."

"Every previous campaign," Vharek said evenly, "did not lose a seated member of this Court within its opening months." He let that sit a moment, watching the table absorb it properly this time, Kaelthorn included. "I am not suggesting alarm. I am suggesting we stop calling it an anomaly simply because it is inconvenient to plan around. Skim the reports. Note the names. An enemy who surprises you once has earned the caution of being checked for a second time."

Nobody argued the point outright, though Vharek noted, with the same patient cataloguing he applied to everything, precisely which generals nodded with genuine consideration and which merely inclined their heads to end the conversation faster.

Malgorath, for his part, said nothing at all — already turning his stone over once more, entirely unconcerned with a danger that, Vharek suspected privately, would need to grow considerably larger before it ever crossed the threshold of his attention.
