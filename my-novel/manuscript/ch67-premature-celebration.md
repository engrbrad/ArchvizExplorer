# Chapter 67 — Premature Celebration

The news reached human territory more slowly, and considerably more triumphantly, than the Court's own grim uncertainty.

"The Demon King is dead!" The words spread through Ravencourt's streets like wildfire, broadsheets printed and reprinted faster than the presses could reasonably manage, taverns and guild halls erupting into celebration that hadn't been seen anywhere on the continent since long before Selvane's fall.

"They're saying it's over," Cassian reported back to the workshop, having gone into the city to gauge the mood and returned looking more troubled than the celebratory atmosphere outside might have suggested. "People are talking like the war's already won. Like the whole Demon Kin threat just collapses now that their King's gone."

"That's not remotely true," Mirelle said sharply, looking up from her instruments. "They still have eleven Court generals, an entire standing army, territory they've already conquered. A dead king doesn't undo two years of ground lost."

"I know that," Cassian said. "You know that. Doesn't stop half of Ravencourt from acting like the fighting's basically finished."

Seraphina, present for the workshop's tense, private conversation, shook her head slowly. "Word from the front's more measured, at least. Whatever's happening within their Court, their forces have genuinely stopped advancing — first time in two years. Whether that's caution, reorganization, or actual collapse, nobody on our side knows yet. But it's real. I've had confirmation from three separate fronts."

"That buys us time," Leandro — still absent, still somewhere in the mountains fighting his own private, unknown battle to survive — would have said, had he been present for the conversation. In his absence, the weight of that unspoken observation settled instead on the small circle who understood the truth of what had actually happened.

"We can't tell anyone," Borgrim said quietly, voicing what all of them had been carefully avoiding saying aloud. "Not the celebrating crowds outside, not the guild, not even Doran's party, however much we trust them. If word gets out that a weapon like this exists, and that we're the ones who built it—"

"Everyone wants it," Mirelle finished grimly. "Every kingdom desperate to end this war faster. Every ambitious noble who'd see it as the ultimate leverage. And the Court itself, the moment they understand what actually happened, hunting relentlessly for whoever did this and however it was built."

"So we say nothing," Seraphina said. "We let the world believe whatever story makes sense to it — an accident, an internal betrayal within their own ranks, anything except the truth. And we wait."

"For what?" Cassian asked quietly, the unspoken fear underneath the question plain in his voice. "For Leandro to come back?"

Nobody answered that directly, the workshop falling into heavy, worried silence, five days now since he'd launched into the night sky and no word yet confirming whether he'd survived what he'd set in motion.

"He said he'd come back," Borgrim said finally, gruff but resolute. "I choose to believe that, until proven otherwise."

Outside, Ravencourt's streets continued their premature, unknowing celebration, a continent daring to hope the war might finally be turning — while five people who understood the true cost and cause of that hope sat in careful, frightened silence, keeping a secret that could reshape the world twice over, waiting for a friend who hadn't yet found his way home.
