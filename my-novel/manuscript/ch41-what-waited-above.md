# Chapter 41 — What Waited Above

The climb back to the surface took two full days, the party moving slower now not from danger but from the accumulated weight of everything they carried — supplies, rewards, and a discovery that Leandro suspected would echo through Brindlemark's guild hall for months to come.

They emerged into daylight that felt, after so long underground, almost aggressively bright, and Brindlemark itself greeted them with none of the chaotic, cheerful energy Leandro remembered from their departure. The market square carried a different weight now — hushed conversations, posted broadsheets clustered with anxious readers, the particular tension of a town absorbing news it hadn't yet learned how to fully process.

"Something's happened," Cassian said quietly, reading the room with the same instinct that had once made him invaluable in Selvane's chaos.

They found out within the hour, at the guild hall, from a clerk who looked like she hadn't slept properly in days.

"The eastern front's collapsing faster than anyone projected," she said, voice flat with the particular exhaustion of someone who'd repeated the same grim summary too many times already. "Three more towns lost in the past month alone. Demon Kin forces aren't just raiding anymore — they're holding ground, consolidating it, moving like an actual invasion rather than scattered incursions."

Doran's expression hardened. "Casualties?"

"Bad," the clerk admitted. "Though not as bad as they could be. Some names are making the broadsheets for good reason." She slid a stack of recent notices across the counter. "A swordsman out of Dunmere's garrison held a mountain pass alone long enough for an entire evacuation column to clear — dead by the end of it, but they're calling him a second Voss already. A healer-priest from Thessary's federation has apparently saved entire refugee columns from disease outbreaks in the camps, keeping death tolls far lower than anyone expected given the conditions." She hesitated, then added, almost reluctantly, "And the fire mage—"

Leandro's attention sharpened immediately. "Seraphina."

The clerk glanced up, faint surprise at the familiarity. "You know her? She's been fighting practically nonstop along the western border for months now. Reports say she's lost battles — actual ground given up, towns she couldn't save in time. But here's the strange part." She tapped the broadsheet. "Death tolls in the places she's fought are consistently lower than anywhere else on the front. She loses ground, but she doesn't lose people, not the way other engagements have. Guild analysts are calling her the most effective loss on the entire eastern theater, which is a strange thing to be famous for, but there it is."

Leandro absorbed that with quiet, complicated relief — proof that competence and genuine care could still matter, even wrapped in the shape of retreat rather than victory. He thought, briefly, of her words in Ostwick's camp — this is where the actual work is — and understood now, more fully, exactly what she'd meant.

"Other kingdoms?" Doran asked.

"Same story, different fronts," the clerk said. "Ravencourt's held its borders better than most, but even they're stretched thin. Dunmere's taken heavy losses in the mountain passes. Thessary's coastal position has bought them some breathing room, but their federation council's been arguing for weeks about how much aid to actually commit elsewhere." She paused, then added the one piece of news that had clearly reshaped the room's mood most. "But there's this, too."

She unfolded a fresher broadsheet, ink barely dry, headline bold enough to catch even a passing glance.

DEMON GENERAL FALLS — RAVENCOURT'S CHAMPION CLAIMS VICTORY

"Eleventh seat of whatever council commands them," the clerk said. "Killed three weeks back by Ravencourt's strongest independent adventurer — some kind of duel, according to survivors, though details are thin. Second one of their generals confirmed dead since Selvane fell. Whole continent's talking about it. First real proof that these things can actually be beaten, not just survived."

The guild hall around them had grown noticeably quieter, several nearby adventurers clearly listening in, the weight of the news settling over the room in a way that mixed genuine hope with the sober understanding of exactly how much it had cost to earn it.

Doran folded the broadsheet slowly, expression grim but resolute. "Two generals down. A continent's worth of towns burning regardless." He looked around at his own exhausted, battered team, freshly returned from fifty-one floors of danger most of Brindlemark would never fully appreciate. "We picked an interesting month to come back up for air."

"We should report the new floor to the guild properly," Ilyana said quietly. "But I think that news is going to matter a lot less to people right now than it would have a season ago."

"It still matters," Leandro said. "Just not urgently. The floor isn't going anywhere. This—" he gestured toward the broadsheets, toward the tense, anxious energy filling the hall, "—is."

Cassian glanced at him, reading something in his expression that had been building, quietly, since the moment they'd first heard Voss's name mentioned in that same guild hall months ago. "You're thinking about moving again."

"I'm thinking," Leandro said slowly, "that fifty floors taught me more about my own limits and how to work around them than anything else since I arrived in this world. And I think it's past time I found out what that's actually worth, somewhere it matters more than a dungeon rewarding careful adventurers with coin and crystals."

Doran studied him for a long moment, then nodded, unsurprised. "Wherever you're headed next, Valdez — you've more than earned the right to walk away from this team with our respect, whatever you decide."

"Thank you," Leandro said quietly. "All of you. I mean that."

Outside, Brindlemark's chaotic, restless energy carried on around them, a town still full of adventurers chasing fortune and glory even as a continent slowly reorganized itself around a war that showed no sign of stopping soon — and Leandro, standing amid it all, felt the last of his earlier uncertainty finally settle into something like resolve.
