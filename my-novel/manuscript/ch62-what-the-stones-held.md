# Chapter 62 — What the Stones Held

The workshop greeted their return with a mixture of relief and barely contained anticipation, Borgrim abandoning whatever he'd been forging mid-task the moment he spotted the party approaching, Mirelle already reaching for her instruments before anyone had even properly dismounted.

"You're alive," Borgrim said, relief plain in his gravelly voice, before his sharp eyes caught on Bram's bandaged, foreshortened arm and the easy warmth faded into something grimmer. "Not all of you unscathed, I see."

"We'll fix it," Ilyana said firmly, helping Bram settle onto a bench near the workshop's entrance, Percival immediately resuming the maintenance healing he'd sustained across the entire journey back. "It's already being handled. Slowly. Properly."

Leandro didn't waste time on preamble, retrieving the three dragon stones from his storage ring and setting them carefully on the workshop's central table. Even in the dim afternoon light filtering through the windows, they seemed to hold their own inner luminescence — deep, layered colors shifting faintly beneath a crystalline structure denser and more complex than anything Mirelle's collection had ever contained.

Mirelle actually gasped, a sound Leandro hadn't heard from her in the entire time he'd known her.

"These are..." She reached for one with visible restraint, hands hovering just short of contact, as if some instinct warned her against careless handling. "Ophelia's academic rumors severely understated this. The density alone—" She finally lifted one carefully, holding it up to the light, expression somewhere between scholarly rapture and genuine alarm. "This isn't just more concentrated mana. The internal structure is completely different. Look at this lattice pattern. Ordinary crystals form in relatively simple, repeating structures. This is layered. Recursive, almost. Like the stone itself learned to hold more by folding its own capacity in on itself."

"Can you work with it?" Leandro asked, watching her expression carefully. "Or is it too far beyond what we've been designing for?"

Mirelle was quiet for a long moment, turning the stone slowly, clearly running calculations behind her sharp, focused eyes. "I don't know yet," she admitted, which was, as always, more valuable than false confidence. "The containment principles should scale, in theory. But this is holding perhaps twenty, thirty times the density of our best refined cores. If our current housing design failed catastrophically on ordinary crystals, pushing this into the same structure without significant redesign would be—"

"Considerably worse," Borgrim finished grimly, already examining the stone with a craftsman's assessing eye. "We'd need entirely new containment geometry. Thicker walls, more sophisticated pressure distribution, probably an entirely different triggering mechanism given how much more force this could theoretically release."

"How long," Leandro asked.

"Longer than the last design took," Borgrim said honestly. "Months, at minimum, done properly. Less than that, and I won't put my name on the workmanship."

Leandro nodded slowly, some part of him relieved by the honest timeline rather than frustrated by it — proof, at least, that the team's instincts toward caution hadn't eroded despite the growing pressure of the war outside.

"We have three," he said, studying the stones. "Which means we can afford to fail twice, in testing, and still have a chance to get it right."

"Or afford to succeed once, properly, and save the other two for whatever emerges from studying this one," Mirelle countered. "I'd rather we not treat these as disposable test material. Whatever civilization or creature produced something this refined, we're not going to find more of them easily."

"Agreed," Leandro said. "One for careful, controlled study and containment design. The other two stay secured until we actually understand what we're working with."

The weeks that followed settled into a new, more cautious rhythm — Mirelle's research into the dragon stone's structure proceeding with painstaking, deliberate care, Borgrim reworking the housing design from first principles rather than simply scaling up the previous version, both of them acutely aware, after the island's destruction, of exactly how much could go wrong if arrogance outpaced their actual understanding.

Bram's recovery proceeded in parallel, slower but steady — Seraphina's academy contacts eventually locating a specialist healer capable of proper regenerative work, though the process, they were told, would take months rather than weeks, and would require Bram to remain near the specialist's own facility rather than continuing to travel with the party.

"I'll catch up," Bram said, when the party gathered to see him settled at the healer's care facility, his usual quiet stoicism carrying a harder edge now, tempered by everything the fight at Selvane had cost him. "Don't go finishing this war without me."

"We wouldn't dream of it," Doran said, gripping his remaining hand firmly. "Heal properly. That's an order, not a request."

Leandro watched the parting with a familiar, complicated weight settling in his chest — another cost paid, another name added to the long list of people the war had touched in ways that couldn't be fully undone, even with magic capable of regrowing a lost limb.

Months to heal a single arm, he thought, walking back toward the workshop with the weight of everything still ahead of them. And I'm asking everyone around me to keep paying prices like this, chasing something that might still take years to perfect.

He didn't have a clean answer for that thought, the same way he hadn't had one months ago at Seraphina's fire pass. But he kept walking anyway, back toward Borgrim's forge and Mirelle's careful calculations, because stopping now, with two dragon stones still waiting and a war still grinding on outside, had never once felt like the right kind of mercy.
