# Chapter 79 — What the Night Allowed

They stayed up long past any reasonable hour, the workshop's usual disciplined quiet giving way to something looser and more celebratory than Leandro had seen from this group in months — Borgrim producing a bottle of something strong he'd apparently been saving for exactly this occasion, Seraphina finally letting her guard down enough to laugh freely at Cassian's increasingly exaggerated retelling of their early, disastrous dungeon attempts.

"Floor twenty," Cassian was saying, gesturing with his cup for emphasis, "and I'm telling you, the man phases through a killing blow, drops unconscious mid-ghost, and I'm standing there thinking my best friend just died standing up like some kind of tragic statue."

"I did not die standing up," Leandro said, amused despite himself.

"You absolutely looked like you were considering it, artistically," Cassian said. "Very dramatic. Very wasteful of a good death pose."

Mirelle, seated slightly apart from the louder end of the table, laughed at that — a genuine, unguarded sound Leandro didn't often hear from her, usually so contained and precise even in casual conversation. She caught him noticing and looked away a beat too quickly, something faintly self-conscious in the gesture that he registered without quite understanding, too tired and too pleasantly warmed by the drink to examine it properly.

"You should hear him explain trap mechanisms," she said instead, recovering smoothly. "Valdez has this habit of narrating his own reasoning out loud while everyone else is quietly panicking. Very calming, actually, once you get used to it."

"It's a useful habit," Leandro said. "Panic doesn't solve floor puzzles. Talking through the logic does."

"It's an endearing habit," Mirelle corrected, something warmer in her tone than her usual clinical precision typically allowed, before seeming to catch herself and returning her attention rather pointedly to the drink in front of her.

Cassian, never one to miss an opening that good, leaned back with a slow, theatrical grin. "Endearing," he repeated, glancing between Mirelle and Leandro with open, delighted mischief. "That's a word choice."

"It's an accurate word choice," Mirelle said, with as much dignity as she could apparently muster while visibly fighting down color in her cheeks. "I study things precisely. Precision demanded that word."

"Sure," Cassian said, clearly enjoying himself enormously. "Precision."

Leandro, several drinks past his usual careful restraint and considerably more relaxed than he'd been in months, missed most of the undercurrent entirely, simply smiling at what he took for ordinary teasing rather than anything requiring closer examination. Seraphina, watching the exchange with far sharper attention, caught Mirelle's eye across the table and offered the smallest, knowing smile, which only deepened Mirelle's blush before she buried her attention back in her cup entirely.

It was Cassian, well into his cups by that point, who finally circled the conversation back toward the ability Leandro had spent so long keeping carefully hidden from nearly everyone.

"You know what I think about, sometimes," Cassian said, leaning across the table with the particular loose, unguarded honesty that came only from strong drink and genuine relief at a friend's safe return. "That phasing thing of yours. I think about the sheer, criminal waste of it."

"Waste," Leandro repeated, already suspecting where this was headed and too tired to properly brace for it.

"Man, if I had the ability to become intangible on command," Cassian said, gesturing broadly, "you think I'd be wasting it dodging bear attacks and dungeon traps? I'd be walking through walls. Vaults. Very specific, very private changing rooms, purely by accident, obviously, purely coincidental timing—"

"Cassian," Seraphina said, half-laughing, half-warning.

"I'm just saying," Cassian continued, entirely undeterred, grinning wide. "The moral fortitude on this man. Genuinely inspiring. Tragic, really, all that unclaimed potential."

"It doesn't work on command," Leandro said dryly, though he was fighting his own smile now. "And even if it did, I'd still rather not spend my one uncontrollable superpower on being a menace to public decency."

"See, that's exactly the kind of restraint that keeps me humble," Cassian said, raising his cup in an exaggerated toast. "To Valdez. Walking, breathing proof that having the power to be terrible doesn't mean you have to use it."

"A low bar for a toast," Borgrim said, though he raised his own cup regardless, clearly amused.

"I contain multitudes," Leandro said, echoing an old joke from months ago, and the table dissolved into fresh, easy laughter that carried none of the war's weight, none of the accumulated grief and danger of the past months — just five people, together and safe for one unguarded evening, grateful simply for each other's continued, stubborn survival.

Mirelle, watching him laugh properly for what was probably the first time since his return, allowed herself a longer, quieter look than she'd risked all evening — taking in the exhausted, weathered lines of his face, the genuine warmth he so rarely let fully show, and felt something settle in her chest that she wasn't yet ready to examine too closely, filing it away instead with the same careful, private precision she brought to everything else in her life.
